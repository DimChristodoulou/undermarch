The Drowned Chapel — ExpansionTen new keyed areas for Adventure 01, numbered to continue A1–A5 and B1–B3.

Nothing here is canon yet. Cut freely — each area is self-contained and removing one breaks nothing except where a dependency is explicitly flagged. When you've made your cuts, the survivors get merged into Adventure 01 in one pass.

This expansion is what makes the level-up happen. Adventure 01's spine is 605 XP. Level 2 needs 1000. The areas below, with the exploration award applied, are worth about 410 — which is precisely the gap. A party who explore thoroughly level at the end of Adventure 01; a party who don't, don't. That is the intended shape, so cutting areas has a pacing cost, not just a content cost. Keep that in view while you trim.

Read firstTwo design principles

The Mouth caught a piece of the village, not just a building

Proposed

A6–A8 and A11 exist outside the chapel walls, in the debris field wedged into the Mouth. This is what lets the site grow without pretending a rural parish chapel had a dozen chambers. If you cut this premise, cut A6, A7, A8 and A11 together — the interior areas stand on their own.

The 30° tilt gives every space one of three states

Keeping the states balanced is what stops the tilt becoming scenery. If you cut areas, try to leave at least one of each state standing. Note the downhill column is thin now that A13 is gone — A3 in the main adventure is carrying that weight.

How these payThe Exploration Award

20 XP per area found and engaged with

Applied in Adventure 01

Not for walking past it. Not for a Perception check in the corridor. The award lands when the party go in and do something with it — search the cottage, clear the well-pipe, haul the stores, get into the tower.

Nine of the ten areas below qualify: A6, A7, A8, A9, A10, A12, B1a, B2a, B2b. A11 does not — it pays 80 XP as an encounter instead, and stacking both would overpay it.

A6–A8Outside, in the debris field

A6 · The Churchyard Slab

AreaCuttable

State: uphill, open. Time cost: 1 hour to search properly. XP: 20 explored.

Access: reached off the descent line before the party ever enters the chapel — Athletics DC 13 to swing across to it, or free if they have Orrow rigging for them.

Read-aloud sense: a wedge of graveyard came down with the building and tipped against the Mouth wall. Headstones stand at angles that are almost right, which is worse than if they had all fallen. Two coffins were shaken out of the soil on the way down and split open. The turf is still green in patches, two months on, because nothing down here has told it to die yet.

What's here. Nothing attacks. This is a tableau, and its function is to make the Vigil's problem concrete before the party meets the dead in A2: Cotter needs to know who is who, the markers have separated from the plots, and now nobody can say which grave is which. Religion DC 15 recognises the burial order has been destroyed; a character who reports this to Cotter honestly gets her regard whether or not they bring anything up.

Loot.

  • Sexton's kit — shovel, pick, coffin rope, 60 ft. Free, heavy, immediately useful.
  • Block and tackle from the grave hoist, intact. This is the Crafting DC 15 hoist that the body recovery in A2 requires — found, rather than improvised. Cuts the recovery from 6 hours to 4.
  • Loose coin from the split coffins, 8 sp, and taking it is exactly the kind of thing Cotter will hear about.

Dependency: if you cut A6, the Crafting DC 15 hoist improvisation in A2 stands as written in Adventure 01 and nothing needs changing.

A7 · The Priest's Cottage (half of it)

AreaCuttableVigil payoff

State: sideways. Time cost: 1 hour, plus 30 minutes if they force the desk. XP: 20 explored, plus 30 for the register.

Access: the cottage sheared in half; the surviving half hangs off the slab with its floor now a wall. Entry is a crawl up through the hearth opening — Athletics DC 15, or Acrobatics DC 15 to squeeze. A failure is not a fall; it is a stuck character and a wasted 10 minutes.

Read-aloud sense: a room where every object has slid into one corner and stayed there for two months. A bed, a chair, a shelf of nothing valuable, all piled against what used to be the north wall. The fire was banked when it happened. The ash is still in a neat cone.

Loot.

  • The parish register. A bound book listing every soul in the congregation. It names all forty-one. This is the single most valuable object in the adventure and it is worth nothing to anyone but the Vigil. With it, Cotter can perform the rites properly instead of over anonymous bodies. Without it, she can't, and she knows it.
  • The poor-box strongbox — Thievery DC 18 to pick, or Athletics DC 20 to break, or find the key in the ash cone with a Perception DC 17. Contains 14 gp in mixed coin. Parish charity money, and everyone will know that's what it is.
  • A bottle of genuinely good brandy. Worth 2 gp, worth considerably more handed to Cotter at hour twenty.

GM note. The register is the cleanest test of the adventure's actual question. It is a book. It is not shiny. The Compact does not want it. A party that carries it up is telling you what kind of campaign they want.

A8 · The Well-head

AreaHazardCuttable

State: sideways, and windy. Time cost: 30 minutes to identify, 2 hours to traverse. XP: 20 explored.

Access: the village well came down as a stack of stone rings, now lying at an angle like a dropped pipe, one end open to the Mouth and the other buried in fill. Survival DC 15 or Perception DC 17 notices the air moving through it hard.

What it is. An alternate route into the cave system behind the chapel that bypasses the sacristy and the altar-breach entirely. Clearing the fill takes two hours with the sexton's tools from A6, four without. It comes out in B1 about eighty metres past the breach — after the Blackdamp Pocket, so a party that uses it skips that hazard as well.

Hazard. This is a constriction feeding directly off the Throat's draft, which is the Venturi case in the Gameplay page's Throat Squall notes. While anyone is inside the pipe, run a single surge of the descent squall from Adventure 01 with two changes: the DC rises to 21, and there is nowhere to be pushed — a failure means being scraped along the stone for 1d6+3 bludgeoning instead of being moved. No Torn Debris; there is nothing loose in a stone pipe. One surge only, so no separate XP.

GM note. The value of A8 is that it gives a party who bounce off the nave fight a way to still reach Act 3. It is a pressure valve. Cut it if you'd rather the chapel be mandatory, but then be ready for the adventure to stall if the shamblers go badly.

Pacing cost of keeping it: it lets the party skip the Blackdamp Pocket, which is 20 XP they then don't earn. Net effect on the level-up is roughly zero.

A9–A11The dry side, and the tower

A9 · The Undercroft

AreaCuttableFaction lever

State: uphill, dry. Time cost: 1 hour. XP: 20 explored, no accomplishment — it pays in loot and leverage.

Access: a burial vault under the chancel, which is now the high end, so the water never reached it. The stair down is now a stair sideways. The door is swollen shut — Athletics DC 16 to force, and the noise carries to A2 if the nave hasn't been cleared.

Read-aloud sense: the only room in the building that smells of dust instead of water. Six older burials in wall niches, generations of them, undisturbed and still in order. Someone came down here in a hurry two months ago and did not have time to be careful about it.

Loot.

  • The church plate, shoved into a niche when the shaking started — chalice, two patens, a censer chain. 25 gp of silver.
  • The reliquary. A hinged box holding a fingerbone and a name nobody at the Fallwater recognises. Materially worth 12 gp. Practically, it is the strongest bargaining chip in the adventure: the Vigil wants it returned as a matter of doctrine, the Compact wants it registered and inventoried, and the Sounding Line wants to know why a village this new had a relic this old. All three reactions are worth more than the silver.

Open question this raises. Where did a two-generation frontier parish get an old relic? You do not need an answer at the table. Say the name is unfamiliar and let it sit.

A10 · The Charity Store

AreaCuttableSupply

State: uphill, dry. Time cost: 30 minutes to find, 3 hours to haul out. XP: 20 explored, plus 30 for getting the stores up.

Access: a lean-to store off the chancel, above the waterline. The door opens downhill, so it swings hard once unlatched — Reflex DC 13 or take 1 bludgeoning and be knocked prone on the tilt. Trivial, and it makes the tilt bite once more.

What's here. The parish's winter charity: sacks of grain, a cask of salt pork, folded blankets, a crate of tallow candles, four flasks of lamp oil.

Why it matters more than the silver. The Lip is two months from winter with a ward full of sick people and a garrison rationing. This is not treasure and it is the most useful thing in the chapel. Hauling it costs three hours and takes the winch line the bodies need.

Resolved 2026-09-11: The Ascent now establishes the bottleneck explicitly — one load at a time, six bodies to a lift, forty minutes per round trip. The party cannot bring up both the supplies and the forty-one bodies without burning another clock day. That is the choice, and it is a better one than any fight in the adventure.

Faction reaction. Reyne wants it inventoried and issued through the Compact. Cotter wants it given away at the chapter-house door. Ashfeld does not care and will say so at the worst possible moment.

A11 · The Fallen Belfry

AreaEncounterCuttable

State: sideways, outside the chapel. Time cost: 1 hour. XP: 80 as an encounter, no exploration award on top.

Re-sited 2026-09-11. The first draft had the belfry still attached and near-horizontal, which doesn't survive the geometry — with the tower at the porch end and a 30° tilt, it would be leaning out over the water, not lying down. The tower sheared off at its base on the way down and lies separately in the debris field, jammed against the Mouth wall. That earns the crawl, keeps the jackdaws plausible, and means the bell can roll rather than drop.

Access: a stone tube on its side. Entry at the broken base — Athletics DC 15, cramped throughout, no room to swing a weapon larger than a dagger, and anyone Large simply cannot enter.

Read-aloud sense: the tower came off clean and landed like a dropped length of pipe. Inside, the louvre slots make bars of grey light down its length. At the far end the bell is still on its headstock, resting against the stone at an angle, and the rope is still coiled beneath it where it fell.

Encounter — the roost (Low for six, 60 XP)

1 roost-swarm — reskin Vampire Bat Swarm, Creature 2, using the stat block as printed. Pale, near-blind, feeding on whatever the Mouth's drafts carry in. They have been here since the fall and they are the reason the tower smells the way it does.

Why it's nastier than 60 XP suggests: the tower is a tube. There is no flanking, no retreat past a swarm that fills the whole cross-section, and area damage is the obvious answer in a space where everyone is within 10 feet of everyone else. A party that walks in without a plan will take the hit.

Hazard — the Bell (Hazard 2, 20 XP)

Stealth DC 18 (the headstock is cracked through and the bell is resting on stone, not hanging)
Disable Crafting DC 18 to chock and lash it, 10 minutes

Trigger Any creature moves past the bell, strikes it, or fails an Athletics check in the last 15 feet of the tower — and automatically if anyone pulls the rope.

Effect The bell comes off the headstock and rolls the length of the tube. Every creature in the tower attempts a DC 18 Reflex save. Failure: 2d6 bludgeoning and knocked prone. Critical failure: as failure, and pinned beneath it — Escape or Athletics DC 18 to get clear, 2 more bludgeoning per round pinned.

And it rings. Once, enormously, in a stone tube.

If the bell rings — deliberately, by accident, or as the hazard fires:

  • Every shambler still active in the chapel converges on the nave, arriving in 1d4 rounds. If A2 is already cleared, nothing comes.
  • Roughly thirty seconds after it stops, something a long way back through the altar-breach answers. Not a word. Not an echo. Once.

GM note. Do not explain the answer, this session or next. It is the same thing that took the three from B2, and Adventure 01 is not where that gets resolved. If the party goes looking for it immediately, the passage past B3 is simply dark and empty, and that should be more unsettling than a monster.

Loot.

  • A jackdaw's hoard in a nest in the louvres: 6 gp in loose coin, a silver ring (3 gp), a thimble, and a brass button that does not match anything made locally. The button is nothing. Let a player decide it is something.
  • The bell is bronze, worth perhaps 40 gp, and cannot be moved by six level-1 characters. Its clapper can — 8 gp, 2 Bulk, and worth exactly enough to be annoying to carry.

A12Inside, downhill

A12 · The Singers' Gallery

AreaCuttableTactical

State: downhill, partly collapsed. Time cost: 30 minutes. XP: 20 explored.

Access: the loft tore loose and now lies at a shallower angle than the floor, propped over the pew heap. Athletics DC 14 to get up onto it.

Why it's worth keying. It is high ground over the nave, ten feet above the shamblers and out of their reach, reachable before the fight if the party scouts. A party that finds it first gets a genuine tactical advantage they earned by looking around. A party that doesn't gets the fight as written. That asymmetry is the whole point — do not hint at it.

Interaction with the Slipping Pew Heap. The gallery is propped on the heap. If the heap shifts while anyone is up there, the gallery drops with it: same DC 17 Reflex save, and anyone on the gallery who fails lands in the water among the shamblers. Standing on the free win has a price.

Loot. A bundle, tied with cord, tucked under the front rail: someone's belongings, gathered and knotted for carrying, and never picked up. Spare clothes, a wooden flute, 4 sp, and a letter half-finished to somebody at Xynnar. It goes nowhere. It is just a person.

B1a–B2bBehind the altar-breach

B1a · The Latrine Cut

AreaCuttable

Time cost: 15 minutes. XP: 20 explored. Access: a side scrape off B1, obvious to anyone with a light. Place it before the Blackdamp Pocket, so the party have a reason to be paying attention to side passages by the time the air goes bad.

A pit, dug with a chapel candlestick, and used for weeks. Above it, scratched into the rock, an arrow pointing back toward the chapel.

What it tells the party, for free. Three people lived out here on purpose. They dug a latrine, away from where they slept, and marked the way home. That is not panic. That is somebody who intended to keep living here. It reframes B2 before they reach it — this was a household, not a hiding place.

Fifteen minutes for 20 XP makes this the best-value area in the expansion by a wide margin. That is fine — it is also the smallest and the least interesting to find. Don't be tempted to price it down; the award is for engaging with the place, not for how long it took.

B2a · The Fishing Pool

AreaCuttableLine hook

Time cost: 1 hour to search the silt properly. XP: 20 explored, plus 10 for the marker. Access: at the far edge of the B2 camp; a still pool fed by seepage.

Read-aloud sense: pale blind fish, hand-length, unbothered by light because they have never had a reason to develop an opinion about it. A line of woven cord is still tied to a rock, baited, set, and never checked.

Loot. Perception DC 17 or a deliberate search of the silt: a Sounding Line survey marker, brass, stamped with a depth and a sequence number.

Why it matters. The marker is from a descent that is not in any Sounding Line log. Ashfeld will react to it more strongly than to anything else the party bring her, including the mark at B3. She will write it up immediately. That report will also fail to be logged, and this time she will notice the pattern.

This is the strongest single addition here for campaign purposes, because it turns Ashfeld's dangling thread into something the party watched happen rather than something they were told about. The 10 XP is deliberately cheap — it pays off later, not now.

B2b · The Buried Cache

AreaCuttable

Time cost: 30 minutes. XP: 20 explored. Access: Perception DC 18 at the camp, or automatic if the party think to ask where three people would put what they couldn't carry.

A shallow scrape under a flat stone, packed and covered. Inside: a wrapped bundle of the chapel's remaining lamp oil, a knife, and a folded page from the parish register with a short note on the back in a shaky hand.

The note. Keep it practical and incomplete — a direction, a rationing figure, and one unfinished sentence about something they had started hearing. Do not let it name anything. Do not let it explain B3. If a player asks whether the writer knew they were about to be taken, the honest answer is that the note doesn't read like it.

GM warning. This is the area most likely to over-explain the adventure's ending. If you cut one thing from the B-section, cut this before B2a.

Dependency: the torn register page only works if A7 is in play. Without it, make the note a page from a hymnal instead.

The cost of looking aroundTime & the Pressure Clock

Proposed rule: ten hours below, or a day burns

Proposed · needs your ruling

Ten areas are only meaningful if exploring them costs something. Proposal:

  • The party can spend roughly ten hours below in one descent before the winch crews stand down and they must come up or camp.
  • Every full descent that does not complete the recovery advances the pressure clock one day. More sick, more dead, at the Lip.
  • Full clearance of every area in this document, plus the body recovery, plus Act 3, does not fit in one descent. That is intentional.

Running totals if you keep everything: A6 1h, A7 1.5h, A8 2.5h, A9 1h, A10 3.5h, A11 1h, A12 0.5h, B1a 0.25h, B2a 1h, B2b 0.5h — 12.75 hours of optional content against a 4–6 hour mandatory spine, plus the haul itself.

Say the prices out loud. "Searching the cottage properly is about an hour" before they commit, every time. A clock the players can't see isn't pressure, it's just a penalty you apply later.

The ten-hour figure is still a placeholder. Adventure 01's clock runs in days, and the Ascent now adds forty minutes per lift on top — seven lifts for the bodies alone. That is nearly five hours of hauling before anyone explores anything. Worth doing the arithmetic properly before you fix the number.

935 is close enough to be annoying rather than dramatic. Three ways to close the last 65, in the order I'd prefer them:

  • Raise the exploration award to 25 XP. Nine areas becomes 225 instead of 180, total 980. Still twenty short, but within a single GM judgement call on the night.
  • Add a moderate accomplishment for the reliquary — 30 XP for recovering it and placing it with a faction rather than selling it. Total 965. I left it out because A9 is already well paid, but it is a defensible reward for a real decision.
  • Make the completion award scale. 80 XP for finishing the job, 100 if all forty-one came up. Total 955, and it prices the Ascent choice into the XP as well as the fiction.

My preference is the third, because it pays for the thing the adventure is actually about, and because a party who cut the sling should finish slightly short. Any two of the three overshoot 1000, which is fine — XP above the threshold carries.
