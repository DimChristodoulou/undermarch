GM Overview — the truth, in one place

The Fallwater exhales. Warm wet air rises up the Throat day and night, and what the Vanishing Waterfall mists away comes back as the permanent fog over the Lip.

Forty metres down the shaft, the Drowned Chapel is wedged into a Mouth at an angle. Forty-one members of its congregation are lying in standing water in its low corner. Nineteen days ago a small rockfall widened a crack in the chapel's downhill wall. Contaminated water now seeps out onto the open shaft face, where the updraft tears it into droplets and carries it up to the Lip, where it condenses into the fog that four hundred people breathe.

Three of the chapel's congregation survived the fall, barricaded themselves in the sacristy, and counted thirty-one days on the plaster. Then they left through the altar-breach into the caves behind, deliberately, taking the lamp oil. They made a camp four hundred metres in and lived there for weeks.

Then a giant wasp came. It paralysed all three, dragged them to a sheer drop at the far end of the passage, carried them down to its larder, and laid eggs in them. That was four days before the party's first descent. The lamp oil is still sitting by the cold fire.

The passage itself is a separate secret. At some point in the last two months, insectoid scouts came up it and cut a mark into the chamber wall. In their numbering it reads Passage 26 — a route to the surface. The scouts and the wasp have nothing to do with each other. They used the same road.

What the party can actually learn: the camp, the drag-marks, the drop, the wasp — and, if they are fast, the three themselves, alive (B4). What they cannot learn is what cut the mark or what it says. The mark is the First Sign and it stays unexplained. The wasp is just a wasp, which is exactly what most people at the Lip will want to believe about all of it.

The Pressure Clock

Run this visibly. Days pass when the party rests, rigs, or travels. Tell them what a day costs.

DayState of the Lip
126 sick, 3 dead. The Vigil's chapter-house is a ward. Boiling order still in force and still useless.
3The ward runs out of floor. Patients are being laid in the street, in the fog, which makes it worse.
5First death since the party was hired. If they met that person in the ward, say so by name.
7The Compact's quarantine line at the winch-heads breaks. People try to leave up the cables.
10+Beyond this the adventure has failed on its own terms. Don't kill the colony — escalate to the Compact ordering the Lip abandoned, which is its own disaster.
Hours matter too, now that there are optional areas. The party can spend roughly ten hours below in one descent before the winch crews stand down. A descent that does not finish the job means coming up or camping, and advances this clock one day. The mandatory spine is 4–6 hours; the seven optional areas add 7.75; the body haul alone is nearly five. It does not all fit. That is the point — say the prices out loud before they commit to a room.

Hooks — three factions, three pairs, one winch

Each faction wants the same problem solved for a different reason, and each will ask for something the others would not. With the campaign's six characters, run all three: each pair of characters answers one faction, and the Compact's single winch line makes them one party.

The Compact's Contract

Best for combat-forward parties · Lawful characters · Soldiers and mercenaries

Sergeant Reyne hires the party as auxiliaries — formally, with a written contract, a fixed fee, and a chit redeemable against colony stores. The garrison cannot spare regulars: a third of them are sick, and the rest are holding a quarantine line at the winch-heads that half the Lip wants to break.

What they want: the cause found and stopped. They do not care what it is.

The catch: the contract specifies the party reports to the Compact first and exclusively.

Pay: 8 gp per character on completion, plus a stores chit (see Treasure).

The Vigil's Petition

Best for divine classes · Clerics, champions, oracles · Characters with a duty of care

Warden Cotter approaches the party directly, without money, and asks. The chapter-house has become a ward, the wardens are working in wet cloth masks that do not help, and this one has been awake for four days.

What they want: the cause found and stopped, and any dead recovered and given rites. Both, not one.

The catch: the rites are non-negotiable and will cost the party time in a place where time is dangerous.

Pay: no coin. Vigil standing, healing services, and one minor item from the chapter-house stores.

The Sounding Line's Commission

Best for knowledge-forward parties · Wizards, investigators, rangers · Characters who want to understand

Surveyor Ashfeld has a hypothesis nobody senior will entertain: the sickness is not in the water, it is in the air — and since the Fallwater's air comes up from below, so does the cause.

What they want: data. Solving the problem is secondary to understanding it.

The catch: she is junior, this commission is not authorised, and she is paying out of her own stipend.

Pay: 5 gp per character (all she has), plus her sample kit and her genuine friendship, which is worth more.

The party — three pairs

Nobody meets five strangers at once. Open on three short scenes, one per pair, each ending on its faction's ask, then bring all six together at the winch-heads. Suggested order: the ward, the workshop, the winch-heads, then One Winch.

The Ward — Corvin and Balthor

Answers the Vigil's Petition · the chapter-house · opening scene

Corvin is a patient. He already has Lip Sickness when play opens — run him at stage 1 and let him make his daily save like anyone else; Cotter will Treat Disease on him for free. He is the only character who was here before the Weeping. He grew up in the rim village whose chapel is now in two pieces: half still standing on the Lip, sliced open like a dollhouse, and half forty metres down. He knew that congregation by name, which gives the Vigil's rites a weight no one else at the table carries.

Balthor is not Vigil. He lived in Brannock's desert with a nomadic band of elves who took him in, which is how a human came to live without trouble inside a country whose interior foreigners never see. Cotter has not slept in four days and accepts anyone who will sit with the dying; Balthor has been doing it for a week. That is how he knows Corvin.

The ask: Cotter delivers the petition to both of them, standing over Corvin's place on the floor.

Corvin's sister. She was lost in the Fallwater Undertear, and that story is the player's. She is not in the chapel and not one of the three from the sacristy. No character's backstory resolves in session one.

The Workshop — Andgar and CE-11

Answers the Sounding Line's Commission · the Line's survey chapter

Andgar holds the Diarchy's seat in the Fallwater's Line survey chapter — the seat Corrach-Nom took as the price of the lantern schematics. He is a genuine scholar, and his standing brief from home is to take a particular interest in technique.

CE-11 came up as a specimen, found dormant in a cavern below by a Line-sponsored crew — Nyrel and Vesper's. The Diarchy seat gives Andgar first claim to examine it. If the player has no other meaning for the name, it is the Line's catalogue tag: construct, entry eleven.

The ask: Ashfeld comes looking for hands, and a Diarchy delegate on her unauthorised commission makes it much harder for her seniors to quash it.

GM only. CE-11's construction is legible to Andgar — joins and tolerances that feel like his own guild's work, only far older. This is the Aftermath secret, felt and never explained. Andgar's honest report on CE-11 goes home and is read by the Diarchy's engineering unit (Established Canon → Unknown Knowledge). He does not know the unit exists. CE-11 is now something Corrach-Nom wants.

The Winch-Heads — Nyrel and Vesper

Answers the Compact's Contract · the quarantine line

Nyrel maps for Line-sponsored crews; the Line buys survey data rather than fielding its own expeditions. Vesper is the crew member who goes where the lanterns don't. They were on the crew that found CE-11, and CE-11's first memory is Nyrel's lamp. When Nyrel brushed its mind while it lay dormant, the mind was not empty. It was counting.

The ask: Reyne needs auxiliaries, and these two are the only freelance crew at the Lip with Throat experience who are not sick.

Vesper's origin is open. Two things to avoid when you decide it: making Vesper one of the Hushed, which spends Adventure 02's first contact, and giving Vesper a no-memory arrival from below, which doubles CE-11's hook.

One Winch

Convergence · the winch-heads · end of the opening

Three errands arrive at the same winch-head on the same morning. Bett Orrow will rig one traverse and sign out one set of gear on her own name, and Reyne will log one party. Whoever holds the lift line holds everything below it.

"Three errands. One line. You go down together, or you queue."

The collision: every descent runs under Reyne's contract, so the Compact's report-first clause now binds all six — including the pair working for the Vigil and the pair working for the Line. Each pair keeps its own faction's pay. Who hears about the chapel first becomes the party's first real argument, and they have it before they've been down.

Flowchart

Act 1 — The Lip

The Ward

Surface · one scene · investigation

The fog is waist-deep at the Lip and does not move. It sits in the streets between the scaffolds, thick enough that the winch-towers are shapes rather than structures, and it tastes faintly of wet stone and something underneath that.

The Vigil chapter-house has run out of beds and started using the floor. The sound in here is not moaning — it is coughing, dozens of people, layered and continuous, wet and deep and going nowhere.

A warden hands you a strip of damp cloth for your face without being asked, and does not tell you it works.

Investigation DCs:

CheckWhat it reveals
Medicine 15This is in the lungs, not the gut. Nobody is catching it by drinking — which is why eleven days of boiling accomplished nothing.
Medicine 18Not spreading person to person. Wardens elbow-deep in it for a fortnight are fine; someone who never went near a patient has fallen ill. Exposure isn't about contact.
Society 14Canvassing house to house: the sick cluster tightly around the winch-heads and the dockside quarters nearest the Throat's mouth. At the settlement's edges, cases thin to almost nothing.
Survival or Nature 16The fog is not weather. It rises out of the Throat continuously and has since the settlement was founded. Something in it changed; the fog did not.
Perception 17At the Throat's edge: the updraft lifts a dropped scrap of cloth back up over the Lip. Anything light enough, coming off the shaft wall below, ends up here.
The gradient is the clue. The fog blankets the whole Lip evenly, but the sickness doesn't — it's worst exactly where the Throat's exhaust hits hardest and thins with distance. That points down, into the shaft, for whatever is riding the updraft.

If the party stalls: Bett Orrow at the winch-heads mentions the chapel unprompted if anyone asks her what's on the shaft face. No check required — this adventure should not gate on a roll.

The Catchbasin — ruling it out

~300m · optional · a suspect cleared

Most parties will want to check the water anyway, and they should. The descent is routine, the Catchbasin is loud and cold and unpleasant, and the water is fine — a little mineral, nothing worse.

The noise here imposes a −4 circumstance penalty to Perception checks relying on hearing, and conversation above a shout is impossible.

  • Perception (DC 13) on the ascent: a Mouth at roughly forty metres with a building wedged in it, at an angle, half-visible through the mist.
  • Any winch-hand, asked: that is the chapel from the rim village. There is no reason to go in — it is full of dead people.

The Descent

Rigging the traverse

Preparation · costs one day

Reaching the chapel means going down the shaft and traversing sideways off the descent line to a Mouth nobody has rigged. This costs the party one full day on the pressure clock and requires either:

  • Hiring Bett Orrow — 2 gp, plus she signs out the gear on her own name and is liable for it. She rigs the traverse but does not enter the chapel.
  • Rigging it themselves — Crafting or Athletics DC 16. Success: safe traverse. Failure: the traverse works but the anchor is poor, imposing a −2 circumstance penalty to Athletics checks made on the line. Critical failure: as failure, and the party loses a second day redoing it.
Light, and where it stops. The Throat's major route is lit — sounding lanterns are installed in the shaft at intervals by the Sounding Line, marking the way up to the surface and sideways to the nearest Mouth. The party descends inside that lit corridor and it costs them nothing.

The chapel is not on that route. The moment they traverse off the descent line, they leave the light. From that point on they carry their own, and the draft's DC 20 flat check applies to every open flame. Make the last lantern behind them an explicit image — it is both a good beat and a fair warning.

The traverse itself: Athletics DC 15 to cross hand-over-hand. On a failure the character slips and is caught by their line, taking no damage but ending prone at the Mouth's edge. The Standing Draft applies throughout: −2 circumstance penalty to Perception (hearing), and non-magical open flames need a DC 20 flat check each round or go out.

Hazard — Throat Squall (Hazard 3, complex, 80 XP)

The party's first lesson, and the one that should stick: down here the environment is the adversary. Full version at Gameplay → Throat Squall; this is the descent-scaled version.

Stealth DC 20 (a pressure drop in the ears, a rising howl, a few seconds' warning)
Disable Survival DC 20 to read the warning and brace the party, or Athletics DC 20 to snap-secure a line

Surge — fires on rounds 1, 3 and 5 of the traverse, then passes. Each creature on the line attempts a DC 19 Fortitude save.

Success Pushed 5 feet along the draft (upward).
Failure 1d6+3 bludgeoning and pushed 15 feet upward.
Critical Failure 2d6+6 bludgeoning, pushed 30 feet, and DC 19 Reflex or lose handhold — caught by the safety line, dangling, requiring an Athletics DC 15 to recover position.

Torn Debris — on each surge, one random creature also takes 2d6 bludgeoning from grit and slate torn off the shaft wall, DC 19 Reflex basic save.

The Fallwater exhales, so the push is upward. The line is the only reason any of this is survivable — say that out loud before the first pulse. A character who unclips for any reason has made a decision, not a mistake.

Gentler option: run it as Hazard 2 (60 XP) — DC 17 saves, 1d6 / 2d6 damage, no Torn Debris, two surges instead of three. Use this if the party is genuinely fragile or short-handed.

Act 2 — The Drowned Chapel

The chapel is lying at roughly thirty degrees, wedged into the Mouth. Throughout: the floor is tilted. Downhill movement is normal; uphill movement is difficult terrain; any creature knocked prone slides 10 feet downhill — toward the water and the dead.

Reading the keys. A1–A5 are the spine and every party will see them. A9–A12 are optional — they are there to be found, they cost time, and they are where the exploration XP lives. Areas are listed in the order a party physically encounters them, not in numerical order.

The tilt sorts them. Uphill (A9, A10) is dry and preserved. Downhill (A3, A12) is flooded and rotten. Sideways (A11) means crawling through what used to be a wall.

The chapel in cross-section

A1The Breach — entry

Where the chapel's roofline broke open against the Mouth. A gap the width of two people, jagged with slate and splintered rafter.

The chapel is lying on its side and it has not noticed. Pews have slid down the tilted floor and stacked against the low wall in a splintered heap. A font has emptied itself in a long dried streak across the flagstones. Somewhere above, a bell you cannot see shifts and does not quite ring.

Water runs down every wall, constant and warm, and gathers in the low corner where everything else has gathered. That is where the congregation is.

They are sitting in it. Two months of it.

Perception DC 12: the smell here is much stronger than at the Lip. Anyone who made the Medicine 15 check in Act 1 connects it immediately.

Before they go in. From the ledge at the breach, the whole debris field is visible: the chapel wedged at its angle, and the bell-tower lying separately forty feet off, sheared clean at the base. That tower is A11, and reaching it does not require entering the chapel at all.

A11The Fallen BelfryOptional · sideways · 1 hour

The tower sheared off at its base on the way down and lies separately in the debris field, jammed against the Mouth wall like a dropped length of pipe.

Access: entry at the broken base — Athletics DC 15. Cramped throughout: no room to swing a weapon larger than a dagger, and anyone Large simply cannot enter.

Inside, the louvre slots make bars of grey light down the tower's length, and the air is thick and sour with something that has been living in here.

At the far end the bell is still on its headstock, resting against the stone at an angle that no longer has anything to do with how a bell is supposed to hang. The rope is coiled beneath it where it fell.

Encounter — the roost (Low for six, 60 XP)

1 roost-swarm — see Creatures. Pale, near-blind, feeding on whatever the Mouth's drafts carry in. They have been here since the fall and they are the reason the tower smells the way it does.

Why it's nastier than 60 XP suggests: the tower is a tube. There is no flanking, no retreat past a swarm that fills the whole cross-section, and area damage is the obvious answer in a space where everyone is within 10 feet of everyone else. A party that walks in without a plan will take the hit.

Hazard — the Bell (Hazard 2, 20 XP)

Stealth DC 18 (the headstock is cracked through and the bell is resting on stone, not hanging)
Disable Crafting DC 18 to chock and lash it, 10 minutes

Trigger Any creature moves past the bell, strikes it, or fails an Athletics check in the last 15 feet of the tower — and automatically if anyone pulls the rope.

Effect The bell comes off the headstock and rolls the length of the tube. Every creature in the tower attempts a DC 18 Reflex save. Failure: 2d6 bludgeoning and knocked prone. Critical failure: as failure, and pinned beneath it — Escape or Athletics DC 18 to get clear, 2 more bludgeoning per round pinned.

And it rings. Once, enormously, in a stone tube.

If the bell rings — deliberately, by accident, or as the hazard fires:

  • Every shambler still active in the chapel converges on the nave, arriving in 1d4 rounds. If A2 is already cleared, nothing comes.
  • Roughly thirty seconds after it stops, something a long way back through the altar-breach answers. Not a word. Not an echo. Once.
Do not explain the answer yet. It is the giant wasp in its larder at B4 — one long drone of wings, carried up the passage. A bell is the loudest thing it has ever heard. Let the party find out what it was when they reach the drop, not before. If the bell rang, the wasp is awake: at B4 it starts in the air and the party get no first move on it.

Loot. A jackdaw's hoard in a nest in the louvres: 6 gp in loose coin, a silver ring (3 gp), a thimble, and a brass button that does not match anything made locally. The button is nothing. Let a player decide it is something. The bell itself is bronze, worth perhaps 40 gp, and cannot be moved by six level-1 characters; its clapper can — 8 gp, 2 Bulk, worth exactly enough to be annoying to carry.

XP: 80 as an encounter. No exploration award on top.

A2The Nave

The main body of the chapel. Forty-one bodies in the low corner, half-submerged in standing water perhaps a foot deep. Pews stacked against the downhill wall. The font, empty, wedged against a pillar.

How they died, stated plainly. The forty-one died in the fall. Medicine DC 15 confirms it: crushing and blunt trauma consistent with a single violent event, all of it ante-mortem, none of the signs of starvation, drowning, suffocation or anything stranger. The three who survived survived by luck and nothing else — they were standing in the one part of the building that landed well. Give this freely; every party will check, and the answer being ordinary is what makes the rest of the adventure unsettling.

Encounter — the congregation (Moderate for six, 80 XP)

4 zombie shamblers in the low corner, half-submerged, slow to rise. 2 more emerge from under the pew-heap on round two.

Total budget: 6 shamblers = 120 XP = Moderate for six level-1 characters. Award 80 XP.

Upscale to Severe: 9 shamblers = 180 XP. Use this if the party arrives rested, fully equipped and unbloodied, and if you are comfortable with PF2e level 1 being genuinely lethal. Bring the extra three up out of the water on round three rather than adding them to the opening wave.

Terrain: the tilt (above). The standing water is difficult terrain. Shamblers in the water start prone and must Stand, which the party can exploit if they think of it.

Tactics: shamblers are permanently slowed 1, cannot use reactions, and have no tactics. They move toward the nearest living thing and Grab. That is all. The danger is entirely positional — being dragged downhill into the water with four of them.

No-undead alternative (Moderate for six, 80 XP): 2 rat swarms plus 2 giant rats = 120 XP, feeding. Mechanically similar, tonally very different — pick based on how supernatural you want session one to feel.

Hazard — the Slipping Pew Heap (Hazard 1, simple, 15 XP)

The pew mass against the downhill wall is wedged, not settled. It is holding several tonnes of oak at an angle against nothing in particular.

Stealth DC 15 (it creaks under weight before it goes)
Trigger A creature critically fails an Acrobatics or Athletics check on or beside the heap, a shambler emerges from beneath it, or any Large or heavier force strikes it.

Effect The heap shifts. Every creature on or adjacent to it attempts a DC 17 Reflex save. Failure: slide 20 feet downhill into the standing water, prone. Critical failure: as failure, and take 1d6 bludgeoning from the timber coming with them.

Disable Crafting DC 16 to chock it with broken timber, 10 minutes.

This is what turns the nave from a room into terrain. It is also the fairest possible punishment for fighting on top of a woodpile — and note that A12 is propped on it.

Solving the sickness: the bodies must come out of the standing water, and the crack must be sealed or the pool drained away from the shaft face. That is the fix. It is not heroic, it takes hours, and the Vigil will want it done a specific way. Forty-one bodies, a tilted floor, and a rope hoist.

  • The work: 6 hours with the whole party. Halved with a hoist rigged (Crafting DC 15). Vigil rites add 2 hours and require someone trained in Religion, or Warden Cotter present.
  • Sealing the crack: Crafting DC 16 with materials on hand (pew timber, slate). Failure means it holds for a week and then doesn't — a hook, not a punishment.
  • Getting them up is a separate problem and it is the adventure's last act — see The Ascent.

A12The Singers' GalleryOptional · downhill · 30 minutes

The loft tore loose and now lies at a shallower angle than the floor, propped over the pew heap. Athletics DC 14 to get up onto it.

Why it's worth keying. It is high ground over the nave, ten feet above the shamblers and out of their reach, reachable before the fight if the party scout. A party that finds it first gets a genuine tactical advantage they earned by looking around. A party that doesn't gets the fight as written. That asymmetry is the whole point — do not hint at it.

And it has a price. The gallery is propped on the pew heap. If the heap shifts while anyone is up there, the gallery drops with it: the same DC 17 Reflex save, and anyone on the gallery who fails lands in the water among the shamblers. Standing on the free win is not free.

Loot. A bundle, tied with cord, tucked under the front rail: someone's belongings, gathered and knotted for carrying, and never picked up. Spare clothes, a wooden flute, 4 sp, and a letter half-finished to somebody at Xynnar. It goes nowhere. It is just a person.

XP: 30 explored.

A3The Low Corner

Where everything has migrated. Hymnals, candlesticks, coins from the collection box, and the dead. The water here is a foot deep and warm and this is where the crack is.

Perception DC 15: the crack in the downhill wall, and the fact that water is actively moving through it rather than pooling. Crafting or Survival DC 14: the crack is recent — the stone is fresh-broken, not weathered. Something shifted here in the last few weeks.

Perception DC 16, searching the deepest water: something is wedged under a fallen beam that reads as a body and is not — the priest's cope, waterlogged and heavy, wrapped around a bundle of candles. Let the party think it is a person first. The moment of relief is the only one this room offers.

Exposure: a character who spends an hour or more in A3 without covering their face must attempt a Lip Sickness save. Covering the face with wet cloth gives a +2 circumstance bonus. Mention the wardens' masks in Act 1 so this is fair. Corvin already has it — see the party.

A4The Sacristy

A side door, uphill, jammed. Athletics DC 18 to force, or Thievery DC 17 to pick. Either reveals it was not jammed by the fall — it was barricaded from the inside, a bench wedged under the handle, still in place.

The room beyond is small and dry and it has been lived in.

Someone stacked hymnals into a bed. Someone worked through the communion wine — the bottles are lined against the wall, empty, arranged neatly, which is somehow worse than if they had been thrown. Someone scratched marks into the plaster beside the door, a row of them, counting days. The row stops at thirty-one.

The far wall of the sacristy is gone. Behind it is the cave the chapel is wedged into, and the dark goes back a long way.

CheckWhat it reveals
Perception 14Three distinct sets of bedding. Three people survived the fall, not one.
Survival 16They left through the breach deliberately, not in panic — they took what they could carry, and they took the lamp oil.
AutomaticThe count stops at thirty-one. It has been sixty-two days since the Weeping. They stopped counting halfway, and then they left.
If Corvin is present, he knows the three by name before anyone finds a single piece of evidence, and he will say so. None of them is his sister.

A9The UndercroftOptional · uphill · 1 hour

A burial vault under the chancel. The chancel is now the high end, so the water never reached it, and the stair down is now a stair sideways.

Access: the door is swollen shut — Athletics DC 16 to force. The noise carries to A2 if the nave hasn't been cleared; if there are still shamblers standing, they start moving this way.

This is the only room in the building that smells of dust instead of water.

Six older burials lie in wall niches, generations of them, undisturbed and still in order. Everything here is exactly where it was meant to be — except for one niche, where somebody came down in a great hurry two months ago and shoved things in on top of the dead without any time to be careful about it.

Loot.

  • The church plate, shoved into that niche when the shaking started — chalice, two patens, a censer chain. 25 gp of silver.
  • The reliquary. A hinged box holding a fingerbone and a name nobody at the Fallwater recognises. Materially worth 12 gp, and that is the least interesting thing about it.
The reliquary is the strongest bargaining chip in the adventure. The Vigil wants it returned as a matter of doctrine and Cotter will not ask for it outright. The Compact wants it registered and inventoried, because everything that comes up the shaft is Compact property under the contract. The Sounding Line wants to know why a village founded two generations ago had a relic that old. Placing it deliberately with one faction, rather than selling it, is a moderate accomplishment, 30 XP.

Open question this raises. Where did a two-generation frontier parish get an old relic? You do not need an answer at the table. Say the name is unfamiliar and let it sit. If Corvin is present, the name means nothing to him either, and that bothers him.

XP: 30 explored, plus 30 if the reliquary is placed rather than sold.

A10The Charity StoreOptional · uphill · 30 min to find, 3 hours to haul

A lean-to store off the chancel, above the waterline.

Access: the door opens downhill, so it swings hard once unlatched — Reflex DC 13 or take 1 bludgeoning and be knocked prone on the tilt. Trivial, and it makes the tilt bite once more.

What's here. The parish's winter charity: sacks of grain, a cask of salt pork, folded blankets, a crate of tallow candles, four flasks of lamp oil.

Why this matters more than the silver. The Lip is two months from winter with a ward full of sick people and a garrison rationing. This is not treasure and it is the most useful thing in the chapel.

And it competes with the dead. The Ascent establishes the bottleneck: one load at a time, forty minutes per round trip. Hauling the stores costs three hours of line time the bodies need. The party cannot bring up both the supplies and the forty-one in one descent — one of them waits, and waiting costs a clock day.

Faction reaction. Reyne wants it inventoried and issued through the Compact. Cotter wants it given away at the chapter-house door. Ashfeld does not care and will say so at the worst possible moment. This is the first argument the three pairs have that is genuinely about their factions rather than about each other.

XP: 30 explored, plus 30 as a moderate accomplishment if the stores actually reach the Lip.

A5The Altar-Breach

Behind the altar, the chapel's back wall has broken open against the cave passage it is wedged into. The passage continues into unmapped dark. This is the exit to Act 3, and the only way in — nothing else connects the chapel to the caves behind it.

Act 3 — Behind the Altar

B1The Survivors' Road

The passage runs back into the rock for roughly four hundred metres before opening into a chamber. The trail is easy to follow — scuffs, spent lamp wicks, a dropped shoe. Survival DC 10 to follow; on a critical success, the party also notes that the three sets of tracks are unhurried and evenly spaced. Nobody was running.

Hazard — Blackdamp Pocket (Hazard 2, 20 XP)

Roughly halfway along, the passage dips through a low scrape where the floor drops and the air stops moving. Full entry at Gameplay → Blackdamp Pocket.

Stealth DC 18 (flames gutter low and go orange; the first person through feels stupid before they feel short of breath)
Disable Survival DC 18 to recognise it and withdraw, or Crafting DC 20 to rig a draft-line and ventilate it in 20 minutes

Suffocation [reaction] DC 18 Fortitude. Success: no effect. Failure: fatigued until 10 minutes in clean air. Critical failure: as failure, and falls unconscious and begins suffocating.

Deals no damage and costs them everything else — light, time, and the nerve they had walking in. This is the right note immediately before B2. If the party carried a bird or a lantern they are watching, reward that generously.

B1aThe Latrine CutOptional · 15 minutes

A side scrape off B1, obvious to anyone with a light. Place it before the Blackdamp Pocket, so the party have a reason to be paying attention to side passages by the time the air goes bad.

A pit, dug with a chapel candlestick, and used for weeks. Above it, scratched into the rock, an arrow pointing back toward the chapel.

What it tells the party, for free. Three people lived out here on purpose. They dug a latrine, away from where they slept, and marked the way home. That is not panic. That is somebody who intended to keep living here. It reframes B2 before they reach it — this was a household, not a hiding place.

XP: 30 explored. Fifteen minutes for 30 XP makes this the best-value area in the adventure, which is fine — it is also the smallest and the easiest to walk straight past.

B2The Camp

Weeks of habitation. A fire-scar, bedding, fish bones from something pale they had learned to catch in a pool at the chamber's edge. They were managing.

And then the camp stops.

The fire is cold and has been for a while. The bedding is still laid out. There is a pot beside the ashes with something in it that has long since dried to a crust.

Nobody packed. Nobody took the lamp oil this time.

Perception DC 13: the lamp oil — the thing they deliberately carried out of the sacristy — is sitting untouched beside the bedding. Whatever happened, it happened fast enough that nobody reached for the one resource they'd already proven they valued.

B2aThe Fishing PoolOptional · 1 hour

At the far edge of the B2 camp; a still pool fed by seepage.

Pale blind fish, hand-length, drift near the surface — entirely unbothered by your light, because nothing down here has ever given them a reason to have an opinion about light.

A line of woven cord is still tied to a rock at the water's edge. Baited. Set. Never checked.

Loot. Perception DC 17, or any deliberate search of the silt: a Sounding Line survey marker, brass, stamped with a depth and a sequence number.

Why it matters. The marker is from a descent that is not in any Sounding Line log. Ashfeld will react to it more strongly than to anything else the party bring her, including the mark at B3. She will write it up immediately. That report will also fail to be logged — and this time she will notice the pattern. It turns her dangling thread into something the party watched happen rather than something they were told about.

If Nyrel is present, the sequence number is one the Line never issued to any crew they have worked with, and Nyrel is exactly the character who would know that.

XP: 30 explored, plus 10 as a minor accomplishment for recovering the marker. Deliberately cheap — it pays off later, not now.

B2bThe Buried CacheOptional · 30 minutes

Access: Perception DC 18 at the camp, or automatic if the party think to ask where three people would put what they couldn't carry.

A shallow scrape under a flat stone, packed and covered. Inside: a wrapped bundle of the chapel's remaining lamp oil, a knife, and a page torn from a hymnal with a short note written on the back in a shaky hand.

The note. Keep it practical and incomplete — a direction, a rationing figure, and one unfinished sentence about something they had started hearing. Do not let it name anything. Do not let it explain B3. If a player asks whether the writer knew they were about to be taken, the honest answer is that the note doesn't read like it.

GM warning: this is the area most likely to over-explain the ending. The note wants to become an answer. Don't let it.

XP: 30 explored.

B3The Mark — the First Sign

On the far wall, at about the height of your chest, someone has cut a mark into the stone. It is about a hand across. It is not writing you recognise, and it is not a scratch or a scrape — the cuts are even, deliberate, made with something narrow and very hard, and they meet each other at angles that were clearly decided in advance.

The floor in front of it is soft silt, and the silt is covered in tracks, and you have never seen anything like them.

CheckWhat it reveals
Survival 16The tracks are not a beast's. Too regular in spacing, too consistent in depth, and they show a repeated pause-and-return pattern — something moved, stopped, came back. Searching behaviour.
Nature 18Nothing in any bestiary matches. Not a bear, not a lizard, not a spider, not anything the Sounding Line has catalogued in two months.
Crafting 17The mark was made with a tool — hard, narrow, deliberately shaped, used carefully. Whoever cut it was not in a hurry.
Perception 15A separate trail that the searching tracks never cross: drag-marks for three bodies, running past the mark and on down the passage. Whatever made them is a different thing from whatever made the tracks. They lead to B4.
Do not resolve the mark. Nothing here attacks, and nothing explains the mark or the tracks in front of it. The drag-marks are a different story and anyone who follows the passage finds where they go — the Perception check only tells the party that the two trails are unrelated.

GM only — what it says. It is an insectoid route label: Passage 26, a numbered way to the surface. Nobody on the surface can read it. Never tell the players.

Andgar and CE-11 are the trap here. A tool-cut mark and a construct that predates anyone's guild are the same question, and the players will put them together. Let them. Confirm nothing.

Taking a rubbing of the mark requires paper and charcoal (Ashfeld's kit includes both). This matters for the resolution.

B4The Drop and the LarderOptional · down · 1–2 hours · on a timer

The passage runs on past the mark for about thirty metres and stops at a sheer drop of twenty metres (65 feet).

The drag-marks go right to the edge and stop. There are no marks on the wall below it. Whatever brought them here did not climb down.

Six metres down, a shoe is wedged on a ledge. Grey scraps, papery and light as ash, are snagged on the lip. A stone dropped from here takes a long time to land.

Nature DC 15: the scraps are chewed wood pulp — wasp paper — and there is a lot of it. Recall Knowledge DC 18 (Nature) once they see it names the giant wasp outright.

Getting down. Athletics DC 15 to climb on a rope; DC 20 to free-climb the wet rock, with a fall on a critical failure. The obvious rope is the bell rope coiled under the bell in A11 — long enough, and taking it means dealing with the Bell. Rope bought at the Lip works too. A climber on the rope is off-guard, which matters if the wasp is awake.

At the bottom is a low chamber, warmer than the passage above, and its walls are wrong: grey paper, layered in curves, built out into cells each the size of a person. Three of them are sealed.

Encounter — the wasp (Moderate for six, 120 XP)

1 elite giant wasp, Creature 4 — see Creatures. It flies (40 feet) and the party arrive one at a time down a rope, which is the whole fight: the first two characters at the bottom are alone with it for at least a round.

If the bell did not ring, the wasp is in the nest; the first character down rolls Stealth against its Perception, and on a success the party act before it does. If the bell rang, it is airborne and waiting.

Its venom is an incapacitation effect that ends in paralysis. A paralysed character on the floor is a target for Implant Eggs. Say what the sealed cells are before the first sting lands, so the players know what is at stake.

The timer — are the three still alive?

The three were taken four days before the party's first descent. Each carries a wasp larva and is at stage 2 of the disease when the party arrive. Run it as a fixed schedule rather than rolls:

When the party open the cellsWho is alive
On the day of their first descentAll three
One day later — they went up to the Lip, or camped belowTwo. The third cell is open from the inside.
Two days laterOne
Three days or moreNone. Three open cells, and what came out of them is not in the room.

Getting the larvae out. Medicine DC 18, ten minutes, a healer's toolkit. Success removes it. Failure deals 1d6 damage to the patient and can be retried. Critical failure kills them. Or bring them up alive the same day: Warden Cotter does it at the ward, without needing a roll, and nobody watching enjoys it.

Getting them up. Out of the cells they are paralysed for 1d4 minutes, then weak but able to climb with help. On the Ascent the living take a lift of their own — one more forty-minute lift on a line that is already the bottleneck.

Who they are. Corvin knows them by name (see A4). None of them is his sister. They remember the wasp and nothing useful about the mark — they heard something in the passage some nights, and never saw it.

Why this area exists. It is the only place in the adventure where speed beats thoroughness. A party that explores every room on the first descent may run out of hours before they reach the drop, and the timer is the cost of that. Say the clock out loud when they find the drag-marks.

XP: 30 explored, 120 for the wasp, and an accomplishment for the three — moderate (30) if any come up alive, major (80) if all three do.

The Ascent

The adventure's last act. Everything the party has done is still forty metres below the Lip, and none of it goes up by itself. Forty-one bodies, whatever they salvaged, and six tired characters, on one winch line, in a shaft that has already tried to kill them once.

The haul

After Act 2 · the job is only half done

The line carries one load at a time. Bodies go up in a net sling, six to a lift, which is seven lifts for the congregation alone. Each lift is roughly forty minutes up and back, and the winch is worked by hand from the Lip — Bett Orrow if she was hired, a Compact detail if not, and either way they are people the party can hear but not see.

This is the bottleneck that makes the charity store a real decision. Supplies and bodies compete for the same line, and so does the lead roofing if anyone got greedy in A1. It is also the payoff of One Winch: the constraint that made them one party is the constraint that now forces them to choose between their factions' errands.

The net goes up and disappears into the mist about thirty metres above you, and then there is nothing to do for twenty minutes but stand on a tilted floor in the dark and listen to the winch.

It is the first quiet you have had since you came down here. It is not an improvement.

The second squall

Climax · the shaft, loaded

Fire this on the lift the party are riding, or on the last body-lift if they sent themselves up first. Not on an empty line — the whole point is that it hits when something is hanging from it.

Hazard — Ascent Squall (Hazard 3, complex, 80 XP)

Mechanically the descent squall, with the load as the complication. Same Stealth DC 20, same Disable options.

Surge — rounds 1, 3 and 5. DC 19 Fortitude. Success: pushed 5 feet. Failure: 1d6+3 bludgeoning, pushed 15 feet. Critical failure: 2d6+6 bludgeoning, pushed 30 feet, DC 19 Reflex or lose handhold.

Torn Debris — one random creature, 2d6 bludgeoning, DC 19 Reflex basic.

The Load — while the net is on the line, every Athletics check made on that line takes a −2 circumstance penalty, and on each surge the sling swings: one creature within 10 feet of it takes 2d6 bludgeoning, DC 19 Reflex basic, from a quarter-tonne of net arriving sideways.

Cutting the load — one action with a blade, no check. The penalty and the swing both end immediately.

This is the choice the whole adventure has been building toward, and it should be the players' to make, out loud, under a timer.

Cutting the sling saves whoever is on the line. It also drops six of the forty-one down the shaft, and those six cannot be recovered, and Warden Cotter is standing at the winch-head at the top watching the rope go slack. She will not say anything. That is worse.

Do not signal which answer you want. Do not punish either one. A party that cuts has made the defensible call, and it costs them the rites for six people and a moment they will remember. A party that holds on takes real damage for it and gets to say they did.

If Corvin is on that line, he can probably tell you whose six they were. Use that only if the player wants it used.

If the party sent the bodies up first and rode last, they have already made this decision without noticing, and they get the squall without the load. That is a legitimate outcome earned by good planning — give them the full 80 XP anyway and tell them why.

Coming over the Lip

Closing beat · one scene

They come up into the fog they now understand, at a winch-head with forty-one bodies laid out in rows under sailcloth, and whichever faction they reported to is already waiting.

Play this short. The colony's gratitude is exhausted and undemonstrative. Somebody asks what took so long. Somebody else counts the rows and gets a different number than expected, and has to be told.

Then the resolution.

The Lip Sickness

The respiratory illness caused by aerosolized contamination from A3. Mechanized here so you can run a PC catching it. This is the same thing afflicting the ward in Act 1 — and Corvin already has it when play opens.

Lip Sickness · Disease · Level 1

Saving Throw DC 16 Fortitude; Onset 1 day; Stage 1 fatigued (1 day); Stage 2 fatigued and enfeebled 1 (1 day); Stage 3 fatigued, enfeebled 1, and drained 1 (1 day). Maximum stage 3.

Exposure: one hour or more in area A3 (or equivalent proximity to the aerosol source) without face covering. Wet cloth over the face grants a +2 circumstance bonus to the save.

Recovery: standard disease rules — a Fortitude save each day to reduce the stage. Removing the source (the bodies) does not cure anyone already infected; it stops new cases.

Treat Disease: Medicine DC 16, one hour, grants the patient a +2 circumstance bonus to their next save. Warden Cotter will do this for free, gladly, and it is the only thing she has been able to offer anyone for a fortnight.

Running Corvin: start him at stage 1, roll his daily save openly like anyone else's, and let the fix in A2 mean something personal. Do not scale it to be dramatic. The disease is what it is.

Faction payment

Each pair is paid by the faction that hired it. Nobody is paid three times, and the totals below are per character in that pair.

Resolution — and why it matters

The sickness stops spreading within days of the bodies coming out, and the fog thins back to something people stop noticing again. The colony is grateful in an exhausted, undemonstrative way that suits the tone.

The First Sign is where the adventure actually lands, and each faction fails to hear it differently — which is also where the three pairs discover their employers are not interchangeable:

  • The Compact files it as a beast, or — per their institutional frame — as evidence of a rival nation's covert sappers. Patrols double. Nobody investigates the mark — and this time the Compact is half right, because there was a beast. That makes the mark easier to ignore, not harder. If the party pushes, they are asked which nation they think it is, and the question is not rhetorical.
  • The Sounding Line — Ashfeld is genuinely thrilled and takes the rubbing. Two weeks later she mentions, puzzled, that her report has not been logged and nobody at the chapter-house will say why. If she also has the survey marker, that report vanishes too, and this time she stops being puzzled and starts being careful. First brush with the Line's cautious circle.
  • The Vigil records it faithfully, interprets it as an omen of the Weeping's continuing wound, and is believed by nobody outside their own order.
  • The three, if any came up alive, go to Cotter's ward, and the colony has something it has not had in two months: people who fell and came back. They are the only good news anyone at the Lip hears that week, and nobody asks them about the mark, because they never saw it.
Where this goes. The party has reached Rung 1 — an anomaly, deniable, easy to explain away. Rung 2 is cultivation in the Shallows (Adventure 02). Rung 3 is the Regular Passage — ancient tunnel, recent repair — and that one cannot be explained away at all.

The character threads all stay open: Corvin's sister, Vesper's origin, what Andgar's report sets moving at home, and what CE-11 was counting.

Design notes

  • The three missing people are a rescue on a clock. They were alive, they were managing, and a wasp took them and left the lamp oil. Whether they come home depends on whether the party chase the drag-marks or go back up first. The mark is the mystery; the three are not — keep those apart.
  • Open on pairs, not on a tavern. Three short scenes and a convergence gives every character a reason to be there that isn't "you are adventurers", and it puts the factions in tension before the party has a party. One Winch is the load-bearing scene — the constraint that forces them together is the same one that forces the choice on the Ascent.
  • Run the pressure clock visibly. Say what each optional room costs before they commit to it. A clock the players can't see isn't pressure, it's a penalty you apply later.
  • The bodies are the puzzle, not the monster. The undead in the nave are an obstacle; the actual solution is forty-one corpses and a rope hoist. Let the players realise the heroic part of this job is the mortuary work.
  • The adventure ends on a choice, not a fight. The Ascent Squall exists so the last thing that happens is the party deciding what the dead are worth when the rope is the only thing holding anyone up. Everything before it is setup for that thirty seconds.
  • The environment is the campaign's real antagonist, and session one is where that gets established. Five of the six hazards here are the party losing to a place rather than a creature.
  • No backstory resolves in session one. Corvin's sister, Vesper's origin, CE-11's age — all of it stays open. Session one is where the players learn these threads exist and that the world will not hand them over quickly.
  • Don't gate on rolls. Every critical clue has a fallback: Bett Orrow names the chapel, the smell in A1 confirms the source, the count on the plaster is automatic, the cause of death is a DC 15 anyone can hit. Failed checks should cost time, not the plot.
