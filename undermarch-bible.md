## HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT

Every entry is tagged so you always know what's yours, what's mine, and what's unresolved:

- **[LOCKED]** — Established by you. Canon. I build around it.
- **[PROPOSED]** — My suggestion. Not canon until you approve, reject, or modify it.
- **[OPEN]** — An unanswered question that needs a decision before dependent work can proceed.
- **[BLOCKER]** — An open question that other work is waiting on. Answer these first.

# 1. CORE PREMISE

## 1.1 The One-Paragraph Pitch [LOCKED]

Something tore twenty holes in the world. They opened onto a labyrinth beneath the crust so vast that no one has mapped a hundredth of it — a dark continent under everyone's feet, full of dead cities, unfamiliar ecologies, and things that have never seen a sky. The surface powers have descended, planted flags around each opening, and begun a gold rush into the dark. None of them know that the holes were *made*, deliberately, by something down there that wants up.

## 1.2 What the Players Don't Know [LOCKED]

An insectoid civilization of mages in the deep engineered the openings. Their goal is access to the surface. If nobody stops them, they will assemble an army and invade — within roughly one year.

## 1.3 What the GM Knows That the Players Never Need To [LOCKED]

**[RESOLVED - session 2]** There is no separate external entity behind the Undertears. The insectoids made them; "external force" was shorthand for the fact that the world does not know they exist.

**[LOCKED - session 3] But there IS something at the Root.** A mysterious entity is drawing heat out of the world from below. It did not make the Undertears and has no interest in the surface. It is the reason the deep is cooling, the reason the Stillness is failing, and therefore the ultimate reason the insectoids must migrate upward. It is the campaign's level 17-20 endgame.

**One cause, two symptoms.** The entity drains deep heat. Deep heat powers the Stillness (see 5.3). So the entity is simultaneously freezing the insectoid homeland and dissolving its only protection. This is why the enchantment cannot simply be renewed - the fuel is being stolen faster than it can be gathered.

**[OPEN]** What the entity is, whether it is awake, whether it is the reason the ways were sealed (see 6.1), and whether it can be reasoned with, fought, or only fled.

> **[ARCHIVED - resolved session 2] The Agency Contradiction.** Your brief says two things that don't currently reconcile: *"an external force has torn off parts of the surface"* and *"insectoid mages created the Undertears."* Both can't be the prime mover as written. Three reconciliations, ranked by how much campaign they generate:
>
> **(a) The insectoids are the agents; the external force is the fuel.** They found, bargained with, or drilled into something and spent it to power the ritual. Cleanest, and it makes the external force a *resource* the players can also find, contest, or deny them. **My recommendation.**
>
> **(b) The insectoids are the tools.** Something used them. They believe they authored the Undertears; they didn't. This makes your antagonists tragic and sets up a genuine third-act rug-pull, but it risks demoting your main villain right when the players finally care about them.
>
> **(c) Two separate events, coincidentally simultaneous.** Weakest. Coincidence at this scale reads as authorial mess rather than mystery.
>
> *Resolved in favour of (a) with no external entity at all: the insectoids are the sole agents.*

## 1.4 Elapsed Time [LOCKED - session 3]

**The Weeping was roughly two months ago.** Ten months remain on the invasion clock.

This is a hard constraint and it rules out a great deal. In two months you cannot have: established colonies, cable-cars or winch-towers, surveyed routes, a deep-born population, functioning trade, or completed dams on major rivers.

> **[CRITIQUE - unresolved tension]** The original brief said surface factions have "claimed their territories" and offer "relatively safe haven." At two months those havens are canvas, rope and mud. Two ways forward:
>
> **(a) Keep two months and lean in.** The colonies are *camps*. Descent is by rope and improvised scaffold. Nobody has expertise. Nobody has been below the Shallows. Dams are half-built and uneven, and only the wealthiest or most magically capable nations got one up - which instantly divides the surface into nations that were saved and nations that were ruined, handing you a political map for free. Players get to be among the first to do almost anything, which quietly solves the "why hasn't someone stronger fixed this" problem. **Recommended.**
>
> **(b) Stretch to ~18 months.** Real infrastructure, an established frontier economy, safe havens that actually feel safe. Costs you the rawness, and you must explain why nobody noticed the thermal anomaly.

**Knock-on corrections already applied:** no deep-born generation (3.6); Undertear anatomy is improvised, not engineered (4.1); nobody has measured the inverted gradient yet (3.3a).

# 2. NAMING

## 2.1 The Event [OPEN]

Your term **Undertears** [LOCKED] carries a useful ambiguity: *tear* as in rip, *tear* as in weeping. Folk etymology will drift toward the second. Lean into it — the common name for the event and the scholarly name should disagree, because that disagreement is free worldbuilding.

**Candidates for the common/folk name:**

| Name | Register | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Weeping** | Folk, mournful | Best fit with "Undertears." Commoners say the world wept. Priests preach it as grief or punishment. |
| **The Unroofing** | Plain, blunt | Named from below, not above. Would be what *underground* peoples call it. Consider using it as the insectoid/deep-dweller term. |
| **The Opening** | Neutral, bureaucratic | What a colonial charter company calls it. Good as an *official* name in tension with a folk one. |
| **The Long Groan** | Sensory | If the event took hours and was heard globally before anything was seen. |
| **Thirdnight** | Calendrical | If dust blotted the sun for three days. Becomes a date, not a description. |
| **The Cracking** | Harsh, simple | Serviceable, forgettable. |

> **[PROPOSED] Use three names simultaneously.** *The Weeping* (folk), *The Opening* (official/charter companies), *The Unroofing* (deep-dweller). Which name a character uses instantly tells the players who that character is. This costs you nothing and pays out every session.

## 2.2 The Megadungeon [OPEN]

Naming logic first: **the name in common use is an exonym.** Surface people named it, and they named it while afraid of it and greedy for it. That means it should sound descriptive, slightly proprietary, and a little wrong — the way "the New World" was wrong.

| Name | Why it works | Why it might not |
|---|---|---|
| **The Undermarch** | *March* = contested frontier borderland (Welsh Marches, Spanish Marches). Encodes the colonial-frontier truth of the setting in one syllable. Also puns on marching armies. | Slightly academic. |
| **The Great Hollow** | Plain, ominous, folkloric. Easy at the table. | Generic; several settings use "Hollow." |
| **The Wormward** | Suggests something bored these tunnels. Creepy and memorable. | Commits you to a worm-boring origin you may not want. |
| **The Undersky** | Beautiful inversion — the deep as its own firmament. Fits your sunlit-grove imagery. | Too poetic for a frontier boomtown to say out loud. |
| **The Nether Reach** | Serviceable epic-fantasy default. | Default. |
| **The Sill** | A geological sill is an intrusion between strata. Cold, technical, unsettling. | Too obscure without explanation. |

> **[PROPOSED] The Undermarch**, with **The Undersky** as an in-world poetic/religious term used by people who've lived down there long enough to love it. That split is characterization: newcomers say Undermarch, natives say Undersky. Document title uses Undermarch throughout as a placeholder.

## 2.3 The Insectoids [OPEN]

Do not name them yet. Their name depends on Section 5's design decisions — specifically whether they are a hive (one name, no individuals) or a polity (a name they call themselves, plus several slurs the surface uses). See §5.

**Holding term in this document:** *the Deep Choir* [PROPOSED placeholder] — implies coordinated voice without implying mindlessness, and gives you an out either way.

# 3. THE UNDERMARCH — STRUCTURE

## 3.1 The Archipelago Model [PROPOSED — IMPORTANT]

**Critique first:** "A megadungeon spanning the entire planet" is a scale trap. It sounds enormous, and enormity is the enemy of prep, tone, and stakes. If everything connects to everything, then no journey is meaningful, no region feels distinct, and you owe the players a coherent geography the size of a planet. You will not build that. Nobody has.

**The fix:** the Undermarch is not an ocean of tunnel. It is an **archipelago**. Discrete deep regions — each with its own ecology, its own dead civilizations, its own logic — separated by genuinely impassable gaps: flooded sumps, magma sheets, crushed dead zones, sheer voids, strata of solid unbroken rock a hundred miles thick.

What this buys you:

1. **Prep discipline.** You build one region at a time and each is a self-contained world. No obligation to connect them.
2. **Tonal variety without incoherence.** Region A can be a fungal wetland and Region B a frozen crystalline vault, and that's not inconsistent — they're different places, the way Iceland and Java are different places.
3. **It explains why the 20 Undertears aren't linked below.** Each Undertear opens onto its own region or a shared few. Travel between Undertears is *easier on the surface than underneath*, which is a delicious inversion.
4. **It solves your antagonist's logistics problem** (see below).

## 3.2 The Antagonist's Unique Capability [PROPOSED — SOLVES A REAL PROBLEM]

**Critique:** As written, your insectoids have a power-scaling problem. A faction that can tear the crust of a planet open in twenty places does not need an infantry army. Whatever ritual did that could presumably just delete a city. If they can do the big thing, the small thing is beneath them, and your campaign's central threat collapses.

**The fix, which also solves the archipelago:** the insectoids' unique, defining capability is not raw destruction. It is **connection**. They are the only power in the world that can bore through the impassable gaps between deep regions. They have spent centuries — maybe millennia — building a tunnel network that stitches the archipelago into a single navigable whole. Nobody else has this. Nobody else even knows it exists.

Consequences, all of them good:

- **The Undertears are not their weapon; they are their off-ramps.** Twenty simultaneous surface breaches is only meaningful if you can move an army between them. They can. That's the whole point.
- **The invasion becomes logistically legible.** You can now draw the army's route. Players can cut it.
- **The ritual's cost is explicable.** Boring twenty shafts to the surface at once was a one-time, civilization-bankrupting expenditure. It cost them a generation of mages, or a finite resource, or something they can't do twice. That's why they need soldiers now — *they spent their magic on the doors.*
- **It hands your players a transit system.** Captured insectoid tunnels are the only fast way between distant Undertears. Traveling through enemy infrastructure is tense, thematic, and turns your antagonist's greatest asset into your campaign's map.
- **It gives the players a concrete, escalating objective** that isn't "kill the queen": find the network, map it, seize or collapse nodes.

**Working name for the network:** *the Weftways* / *the Sutures* / *the Stitch* [OPEN]

## 3.3 Depth Bands [PROPOSED — PF2e MECHANICAL SPINE]

PF2e's math is tight enough that you need a legible relationship between *where you are* and *how hard it is*. Depth is the cleanest axis: it's intuitive, it's physical, and players self-regulate against it.

| Band | Depth | Level range | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Threshold** | 0–1 km | 1–4 | Rope-and-ladder country. Colony infrastructure, surveyed routes, sunlight still reaches. Sunlit groves and doline forests live here. |
| **The Shallows** | 1–4 km | 5–8 | Last of the surveyed world. Fungal forests, first ruined cities, competing charter expeditions. Heat becomes noticeable. |
| **The Mid Reaches** | 4–12 km | 9–12 | Beyond any map. The great dead civilizations. Insectoid frontier — first Weftway nodes. Deeper than any real-world borehole. |
| **The Under Deeps** | 12–40 km | 13–16 | Below the crust as anyone understands it. Physics gets negotiable. Insectoid heartland. |
| **The Root** | 40 km+ | 17–20 | Whatever is actually down there. Ties directly to §1.3 and the external force. |

## 3.3a The Inverted Gradient [LOCKED - session 3]

**The Undermarch gets warmer with depth exactly as it should - until somewhere in the Mid Reaches, where the gradient reverses. Below that point it gets colder the deeper you go. The Root is the coldest place in the world.**

Real-world anchors worth knowing, because they make the anomaly legible:

| Fact | Figure |
|---|---|
| Normal geothermal gradient | ~25-30°C per km |
| Deepest mine (Mponeng, South Africa) | 4 km; rock face ~66°C; cooled with pumped slurry ice |
| Deepest borehole (Kola Superdeep) | 12.2 km; abandoned at ~180°C because the rock had gone plastic |
| Continental crust thickness | ~30-50 km |

**Cause:** the entity at the Root is drawing heat out of the world (see 1.3).

**What this buys you:**

- **Heat becomes the scarce resource, not the hazard.** Civilizations down there huddle around thermal vents, magma intrusions and hot springs like campfires. When drawing maps you no longer ask "where are the caves" - you ask **"where is the heat,"** and that generates your settlement pattern for you. Dead cities are found where the heat went out.
- **It is a measurable anomaly.** A party with a thermometer and a rope can discover that the world is wrong. Falsifiable, physical, and found by the players rather than told to them.
- **It gives the antagonists a thermodynamic motive.** Their ecology runs on deep heat. They are not conquering, they are evacuating toward the only remaining heat source - a star.
- **It fills the Root.** The endgame, the sealing mystery and the insectoid motive become one invention.

**Rejected alternative:** conventional heat-as-hazard (hotter all the way down, cold-resistance gear as treasure). Mechanically cleaner and it maps neatly onto level progression, but it is a hazard rather than an idea and it generates nothing else.

## 3.3b Light by Band [LOCKED - session 3]

**Design principle: light must have a source you can lose.** If the deep glows ambiently everywhere, darkness stops meaning anything and you have thrown away your best atmospheric tool. So zone it - each band gets a characteristic light, which also gives your maps and descriptions a signature per depth.

| Band | Light |
|---|---|
| **Threshold** | Real sunlight. Shafts, doline groves, the sun crossing a cave wall over a day. |
| **Shallows** | Bioluminescence. Living light: it moves, it can be farmed, bred and eaten, and it goes out when frightened. |
| **Mid Reaches** | Engineered light. The dead civilizations built lamps and some still work. Light as *ruin* - you read by something's grave goods. |
| **Under Deeps** | Mineral, anomalous, wrong-coloured. Light with no obvious source. |
| **Root** | Nothing. |

### Darkvision [LOCKED - session 3]

Most PF2e ancestries have low-light vision; true darkvision is rarer. In a campaign set entirely underground that creates a split-party experience problem.

**Decision: the deep bands are qualitatively dark.** Ordinary darkness in the Threshold and Shallows, where darkvision works normally and confers a real colonial advantage (who gets hired as guides, who runs deep trade, who resents whom). But something in the **Under Deeps** that darkvision does not penetrate - dark that behaves like a substance rather than an absence.

Why this version: it re-levels the party at depth without nerfing anyone's ancestry choice, it is announced at session zero rather than sprung as a gotcha, and by that level everyone has magical answers. It also makes the deepest bands *qualitatively* different rather than just numerically harder.

**[OPEN]** Name for the unlit dark. Does it interact with magical light? Is it connected to the entity at the Root, or older than it?

## 3.4 Why Hasn't a Level 18 Person Fixed This? [OPEN — MUST ANSWER]

Every setting with a ticking doom owes this answer. Candidates, combinable:

- **They're competing with each other.** The seven surface powers each have a high-level champion and all seven are pointed at *each other*, not the dark. The insectoids' greatest ally is the charter companies' greed.
- **Scale defeats power.** A level 20 archmage is still one person in a labyrinth the size of a planet. Power doesn't solve *search*.
- **They went down and didn't come back.** The first wave of legendary heroes descended in Year One. The Undermarch ate them. Their bodies are dungeon set-dressing and their gear is treasure. This is grim, efficient, and gives you loot with names.
- **The threat isn't legible yet.** Nobody knows there's a war coming. You can't send a hero at a rumor.

> **[PROPOSED] Use all four, weighted toward the first.** The strongest version of your campaign is one where the surface's *inability to cooperate* is the actual antagonist and the insectoids are just the deadline.

## 3.5 Air and the Standing Drafts [LOCKED - session 3]

**Before the Weeping, most of the deep was unbreathable.** Sealed caves accumulate carbon dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, methane and radon. Miners have real vocabulary for this and it is worth stealing wholesale: **blackdamp** (oxygen-depleted, suffocating), **firedamp** (methane, explosive), **stinkdamp** (hydrogen sulfide), **whitedamp** (carbon monoxide).

**Then twenty chimneys were punched through the crust, and the planet started breathing.**

### How the circulation works

With a cold deep the system is more interesting than a simple chimney:

1. **The Mid Reaches are the engine.** The hot band just above the reversal heats air, which rises and vents upward.
2. **Cold surface air is drawn down** other Undertears to replace it.
3. **Root air is dense, pools, and does not circulate at all** - which is why the deepest band stays stagnant and lethal even though everything above it now ventilates.

### Consequences to use

- **Some Undertears breathe in, some breathe out.** An exhaling Tear vents warm, wet, foul air: permanent fog over the Lip, nothing growing near it, the smell of the deep hanging over the boomtown. An inhaling Tear pulls debris, birds and careless people down.
- **Direction can flip seasonally,** because surface temperature drives half the equation. A colony venting its forges up an exhaling shaft is in serious trouble the month it reverses.
- **A breathable frontier is actively advancing.** There is a moving boundary between air you can breathe and air you cannot. It pushes deeper every month. Beyond it you need magic or gear - a natural depth gate that is not arbitrary.
- **Weather underground.** Pressure differentials, fog banks where cold meets warm, storms in the larger voids.
- **Enormous permanent drafts through every Throat.** Descent shafts are wind tunnels. Sound and smell travel absurd distances.
- **The worst squalls happen near the edge of the Stillness** (see 5.3), because moving air meeting motionless air produces violent shear turbulence. **Unexplained wind patterns point toward insectoid territory** - players can triangulate the enemy's borders from weather data before they ever meet one.

Mechanics: see section 13.

## 3.6 Time Underground [LOCKED - session 3]

Michel Siffre spent months alone in caves in the 1960s and 70s and lost roughly twenty-five days of count. Without light cues, human circadian rhythm free-runs long - people drift toward 25-hour days, sometimes much stranger.

**What is true at two months after the Weeping:**

- **Expedition drift.** Parties return from long delves having lost days and arguing about the date. Colony bells and drop-lights at the Shelf keep surface time; below the Verge, nobody knows.
- **No generational drift.** There has not been time. There are no deep-born colonists yet.
- **The fungal calendar is native, not colonial.** Certain fungi bloom on a cycle and pulse light; the peoples who actually live down there time their lives by when the light comes on. This is not yet a colonist custom - it is a **discovery**, something players earn by learning to read it. It also ties directly to the fungus-farming insectoids (see 5.0).

**[OPEN]** What is the fungal cycle's period, and does it match anything on the surface?

# 4. THE UNDERTEARS

## 4.1 Anatomy [PROPOSED]

Give every Undertear the same skeleton so you can improvise new ones instantly, then vary one thing each.

1. **The Lip** — the surface rim. Cracked farmland, collapsed villages, now a boomtown. Whoever holds the Lip taxes everything.
2. **The Throat** — the vertical shaft. Hundreds of meters to several kilometers. The engineering problem that defines the colony: winch-towers, stairs cut into the wall, cable-cars, levitation platforms, rope-and-pulley cages, a bridge spiralling down.
3. **The Shelf** — where the shaft meets the first horizontal space. The actual town. Chaotic, vertical, built on scaffold.
4. **The Verge** — the edge of surveyed territory. Where the map ends and the campaign starts.
5. **The Mouths** — the tunnels leading onward. Usually several. Usually contested.

## 4.2 The Surface Consequence Nobody Has Thought About [PROPOSED — STRONG]

You tore twenty holes several kilometers deep into a planet. **The rivers found them.**

Where an Undertear opened near a watershed, the river now pours into the ground. Downstream: dead farmland, abandoned cities, refugees, war over what water remains. Underground: enormous permanent waterfalls, new flooded regions, whole deep ecosystems drowned or born.

Why this is worth taking:

- It makes descent **non-optional**. The surface isn't going down for treasure, it's going down because the world above is failing and nobody knows how to stop it. That moralizes your gold rush and gives desperate people real reasons.
- It gives you your single best image: **a river falling into the earth, forever, lit by a shaft of daylight.** That's your campaign's cover art and your first sketch.
- It generates political conflict without inventing any: upstream and downstream nations now hate each other.
- It creates a genuine ecological clock running alongside the invasion clock.

### The dams [LOCKED - session 3]

The surface has responded by damming what it can. This keeps the world *a bit* stable - not saved, but not collapsing either.

- **Uneven by design.** Only the wealthiest or most magically capable nations managed a dam in two months, and even those are half-built. Some will fail.
- **This is your political map.** Nations that were saved and nations that were ruined, and everyone knows which is which.
- **Dams consumed the resources that could have funded descent.** A nation with a dam is a nation that came late to the Undermarch.
- **A dam is a chokepoint someone can sabotage.** Breaking one is an act of war - or an insectoid acceleration of the clock.
- **[OPEN]** How were they built so fast? Earth-moving ritual magic is the plausible answer and it implies a class of caster whose price just went up enormously.

**Tone decision:** surface is in *managed* crisis rather than collapse. Descent is still driven by desperation and profit, but the world above is not ending this year.

## 4.3 Prep Discipline for the Twenty [PROPOSED]

You do not build twenty Undertears. You build:

- **One in full** — the campaign's home base. Named NPCs, factions, economy, map, the works.
- **Two in sketch** — a paragraph, a defining feature, one hook. Reachable, mentioned constantly, visited eventually.
- **Seventeen as one-line rumors** — a name and a single strange fact. *"The Ninth is frozen solid and something is singing in it."* That's the entire entry. It does 90% of the work of a fully-built location and costs you nine words.

The seventeen exist to make the world feel large. They will stay rumors for most of your campaign, and that's correct.

# 5. THE ANTAGONISTS

## 5.0 Species Basis [RESOLVED - session 2]

**Decision: civilization, non-negotiable.** Castes, politics, dissent, art, and a defector.

**PF2e sourcing:**

| Option | Fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Formians** | Closest existing insectoid civilization | Real castes (worker, warrior, taskmaster, myrmarch, queen), statted across a level range. Canon leans hive-mind, but upper castes are individually intelligent - small adaptation, not a rewrite. |
| **Thriae** | Best fit for the *mage* half | Bee-folk with prophetic queens and genuine spellcasting traditions. Society organised around oracular authority. |
| **Anadi** | Playable ancestry | Spider-folk with real civilization and craft tradition. Useful for a PC or major NPC of a related lineage. |
| **Mi-go** | Hold in reserve | Too alien and too competent for your main antagonist. Excellent candidate for **the Builders** (see 6.1). |
| **Xulgath** | Precedent, not species | Not insectoid, but PF2e's canonical ancient subterranean empire. Useful model for how a deep civilization rises and falls. |

> **[PROPOSED] Homebrew the species on Formian caste architecture with Thriae magical culture layered on top.** Gives you statblocks to reskin at every level, plus a society where spellcasting is an *institution* rather than a talent - which is what makes "we spent a generation of mages on the ritual" a sentence with weight.

### The fungus-farming hook [PROPOSED]

Real ants and termites farm fungus. If your insectoids are fungus-farmers:

- The deep mushroom forests **are their agriculture** - some cultivated, some abandoned centuries ago and gone feral.
- Your ecology and your antagonist become the same fact.
- A party that learns to read fungal patterns can tell **how long ago the insectoids were somewhere**. That's a tracking skill, a dating method, and a source of dread all at once.

### Reference worth mining [PROPOSED]

Golarion's Darklands run in three descending layers (Nar-Voth, Sekamina, Orv). The deepest contains the **Vaults of Orv** - enormous engineered caverns, some with artificial suns, built by a vanished civilization. That is your sunlit groves and forgotten cities with the serial numbers already filed off. Good reference for what "engineered biome" looks like at scale.

## 5.1 The Central Design Question [ARCHIVED - resolved session 2]

*Answered: civilization. Reasoning retained below.*

**Are they a hive-mind or a civilization?**

This is the single most consequential decision in the project and almost everything downstream depends on it.

**Hive-mind:** one will, no individuals, no negotiation. Pure external threat. Clean, scary, and *thin*. You get a good final battle and nothing else — no diplomacy, no defectors, no moral weight, no scenes. The players never meet a person, only a weather system.

**Civilization with castes:** individuals, dissent, art, grief, internal politics, factions who disagree about the invasion. Everything gets better. Players can negotiate, split them, find a third option, or discover the invasion is a *policy* pushed by one faction rather than an instinct.

> **[LOCKED] Civilization.** Keep the alien physiology — castes, chemical language, radial architecture, incomprehensible aesthetics — but give them politics. The uncanny should come from how they *think*, not from whether they think.

## 5.2 The Motive Problem [RESOLVED - session 3]

**Answer: heat loss.** The entity at the Root is draining the world's warmth; their ecology and their protective enchantment both run on it. They are evacuating toward the only remaining heat source - a star. Original option table retained below for reference.


**Critique:** "They want the surface" is not a motive, it's a direction. *Why* the surface, and why now, after presumably existing down there for ages?

The strongest answer makes them sympathetic without making them safe: **their world is dying and the surface is the only exit.**

Options for what's killing the deep:

| Cause | Flavor | Ties to |
|---|---|---|
| The Root is cooling / going out | Their entire ecology depends on deep heat, and it's failing | §3.3, §1.3 |
| Something is eating the deep from below | Horror. They're not invaders, they're refugees running from a thing you meet at level 18 | §1.3 option (b) |
| Their own ritual poisoned their homeland | The Undertears cost them their world. Tragic and self-inflicted | §3.2 |
| The floodwaters from §4.2 are drowning them | Direct causal link: the surface's rivers are destroying them, so the surface is now a target | §4.2 — **elegant** |

> The fourth option is my favourite because it makes the two sides *mutually* responsible in a way neither understands. The surface accidentally drowns the deep; the deep deliberately invades the surface; both think they're the wronged party. That's a campaign about a misunderstanding neither side can afford to have, and it gives your players something to actually *do* other than kill.

## 5.3 The Stillness [LOCKED - session 3]

**A powerful enchantment holds the insectoid heartland untouched by the Weeping's side effects.** Inside it there is no wind, no current, no draft, no intruding surface air or water - while the entire rest of the Undermarch howls. Their ecology is unfazed. Their homeland is not drowning and not yet freezing.

**It is fading, and it cannot be renewed.**

### Why it cannot be renewed [LOCKED]

The Stillness runs on **deep heat** - it always did, because that was the only abundant energy source down there. The entity at the Root (1.3) is draining that heat. So one cause produces two symptoms: the homeland freezes *and* its only protection dissolves, faster than it can be re-gathered.

**This is the entire motive.** Their real enemy is beneath them, not above. The invasion is what a competent civilization does when it is losing a war it cannot win against something it may not be able to perceive. When the players eventually understand this, *"help us fight the thing at the Root"* becomes a genuinely available move.

### Their actual flaw [LOCKED]

They were right about physics and wrong about **people**. They modelled the air, the water and the thermal load correctly. What they did not model was how fast the surface would come down the holes - they expected fearful primitives to shun the wounds in the earth for a generation. Instead: twenty colonies in two months.

**Contempt for other minds, not incompetence at engineering.** That flaw generates diplomacy, defectors, and an internal faction arguing that the surface has been catastrophically misjudged.

### Using it at the table

- **Crossing the boundary is a sensory event.** The roar simply stops. Nothing moves. It should be one of the strangest moments in the campaign.
- **The boundary is receding, and it can be measured.** Abandoned outposts sit just outside the current line. Old markers stand in howling wind they were built to exclude. **A party that maps the line twice, months apart, has quantified the enemy's timeline better than any prophecy.**
- **Shear turbulence at the edge.** Moving air meeting motionless air is violent. The worst squalls in the Undermarch cluster along the Stillness, so wind data is intelligence data.

**[OPEN]** Is the Stillness one enchantment or many? Who maintains it? Is there a caste whose entire existence is tending it, and what happens to them when it dies?

## 5.4 Structural Questions [OPEN]

- What is their name for themselves? What do surface people call them (probably something crude and dismissive — "chitters," "the crawl")?
- How do they communicate? Chemical, vibrational, sung? Can it be learned? **If it can be learned, that's a skill investment that pays off in a campaign-defining scene.**
- Do they have a religion? What do they believe the surface *is*? (A ceiling? A heaven? A lie?)
- Caste structure: how many, how rigid, is mobility possible? Is there a caste that *wasn't designed for war* and doesn't want one?
- Is there a defector? **There should be a defector.** They are your single most valuable NPC.
- What did the ritual cost them, specifically and namably?

# 6. THE DEAD CIVILIZATIONS

## 6.1 The Era Stack [PROPOSED]

Your instinct — that the underground was once connected to the surface, and sun-averse peoples migrated down — is good and load-bearing. But it needs a **why did it close**, and that answer is one of your biggest lore levers.

Build the dead as a **stratigraphy**: distinct eras, physically layered, deepest = oldest. Then the megadungeon teaches history through *architecture*, and a player who learns to read building styles can date a ruin at a glance. That's a real skill your players will develop and enjoy.

**[PROPOSED] Four eras, deepest to shallowest:**

1. **The Builders** (Root/Under Deeps) — pre-human, non-humanoid architecture. Scale wrong, doorways wrong, no stairs. Nobody knows what they were. Possibly related to §1.3.
2. **The Sunfled** (Under Deeps/Mid) — the migration you described. Peoples who could not bear the sun and went down while the ways were open. Their cities are *recognizably civilized* — plazas, temples, drainage — which makes them the most haunting ruins, because they look like home.
3. **The Sealing Era** (Mid/Shallows) — whatever closed the ways. Fortifications, mass graves, enormous doors barred *from above*. This era is your central historical mystery.
4. **The Aftermath** (Shallows) — isolated survivors. Small, degenerate or transformed, some possibly still alive down there and *very* surprised to see you.

> **[BLOCKER] Why did the ways close?** Candidates: a war; a plague; a deliberate quarantine by surface powers; the insectoids sealed it; something got out once and it was never allowed to happen again. **This answer determines whether the Undertears are a tragedy, an escape, or a repetition.** It is probably the most important single lore decision remaining.
>
> **[PARKED — session 2]** Dim: *"I don't know yet, log this question and we will explore it further."* Revisit in Chunk 5, or earlier if Chunk 2 (antagonists) forces the issue — it may well, since "did the insectoids seal it?" is one of the candidates.

## 6.2 The Living Deep [OPEN]

Your brief specifies you don't want only classic underground tropes — good. Ecosystem seeds worth developing:

- **Chemosynthetic zones** — real. Movile Cave in Romania has been sealed 5.5 million years and runs on sulfur, not sunlight. Fully alien food web, no plants, everything pale. Free weirdness, zero handwaving.
- **Doline forests** — real. Hang Son Doong in Vietnam has collapsed ceilings admitting sunlight, and there is *jungle inside the cave*. Your sunlit groves are geologically legitimate. Draw these.
- **Fungal succession** — not one mushroom forest, but several, ecologically distinct: rot-fed, corpse-fed, one that's farmed and one that farms *you*.
- **Thermal zones** — hot springs, mineral terraces, whole civilizations built around heat like a hearth.
- **The drowned** — post-§4.2 flooded regions, still filling. A region that is actively becoming a sea during the campaign.
- **Aphotic megafauna** — what's the apex predator? What do the locals think it is, and are they wrong?

# 7. THE CLOCK

## 7.1 The Core Tension [CRITIQUE]

**Your two design goals are fighting each other, and you should know it now rather than in month four.**

Megadungeon play is *exploratory*: non-linear, player-directed, slow, digressive, driven by curiosity. A one-year invasion clock is *urgent*: focused, directed, and it punishes digression. Run them naively together and one of two things happens — either the players ignore the clock and your invasion feels like a GM ambush, or they chase the clock and never explore, which wastes the megadungeon you built.

**The resolution: make exploration the method of fighting the clock.**

The counter-invasion is not found on the surface. It's found *down there*: in the Weftway network the players must map, in the Sealing Era's records of how the ways were closed before, in the dead civilizations' weapons, in the insectoid defector's testimony, in the politics of a deep city nobody knew was still inhabited. Every dungeon delve is both a treasure hunt *and* a move against the clock. Then digression isn't a failure state — it's just a different route.

## 7.2 Clock Design [PROPOSED]

Run it as a visible **eight-segment Front clock**. Segments advance on triggers, not on the calendar — this is critical, because calendar-based doom punishes careful play and rewards nothing.

**Triggers that advance it:**
- The players ignore a known Weftway node for an extended period
- A surface faction does something catastrophically shortsighted (this should be most of them)
- The players fail a major objective
- Fixed advancement at long-rest/downtime milestones, so it never fully stalls

**Make it legible.** The players should *feel* it without being told. Escalating signs, one per segment:
1. Tremors at the Verge.
2. Livestock and pack animals refuse the Mouths.
3. Insectoid scouts appear two depth bands shallower than they should.
4. An outlying survey camp goes silent.
5. A Weftway node opens *inside* surveyed territory.
6. A minor Undertear colony falls. Refugees arrive at yours.
7. The Throat of a major Undertear is contested.
8. Invasion.

> **[OPEN] What level do you want the campaign to end at?** A single in-world year is a compressed timeline for 1→20. Options: **(a)** the campaign covers the year and ends around 12–14 with the invasion as the climax; **(b)** the players successfully *stall* the clock and the campaign continues past the year into a longer war, reaching 20; **(c)** the year is the first arc of three. You said level range would follow the world — this is the point where it stops being deferrable, because the depth bands in §3.3 depend on it.

# 8. PF2e MECHANICAL LAYER

## 8.1 The Attrition Problem [CRITIQUE — PRACTICAL]

**This will bite you and most homebrew megadungeon GMs don't see it coming.**

Classic megadungeon play runs on *attrition*: torches burn, rations dwindle, spells don't come back, and the tension is "can we make it out." PF2e is built to defeat exactly that. Focus Points return in 10 minutes. Treat Wounds restores the whole party's HP out of combat for free. Most classes recover fully on a night's rest. The system deliberately assumes each encounter starts near full, because its encounter math is balanced on that assumption.

**Consequence:** if you do nothing, your vast dangerous labyrinth will feel like a series of unrelated fights in a corridor, with a picnic between each one.

**Fixes, in order of how much I'd recommend them:**

1. **Make the pressure environmental, not resource-based.** Heat, air quality, darkness, unstable terrain, corruption/affliction that persists past rests. Afflictions in PF2e *do* survive downtime — that's your attrition mechanism, and it's already in the rules.
2. **Adapt Hexploration into Delve-ploration.** Kingmaker's hexploration subsystem maps almost directly onto the Undermarch: activities per travel period, discovery rolls, supply expenditure. Reskin the hex as a "reach."
3. **Supply as a hard currency, not bookkeeping.** Don't track individual rations. Track *Supply* as an abstract pool. Spend it to rest safely. Out of Supply means no Treat Wounds, no Refocus, no full rest. Now the depth you can reach is a function of what you carried, and that's a real decision.
4. **Use the Victory Point / Influence subsystems** for faction and colony politics. They're in GM Core, they're good, and this campaign is 40% politics whether you plan it or not.
5. **Do not nerf Treat Wounds by fiat.** Players hate it and it just makes combat swingier. Constrain *where* they can rest, not *whether* healing works.

## 8.2 Light and Darkvision [PROPOSED]

Most PF2e ancestries have low-light vision; genuine darkvision is rarer. In a campaign set entirely underground, this is not a footnote — it is one of your most powerful atmospheric tools *and* a balance question.

- **Ancestry choice becomes a setting statement.** Dwarves, orcs, and similar have a colonial advantage. That has political consequences: who gets hired as guides, who runs the deep trade, who resents whom.
- **Light attracts things.** A party carrying lanterns is a moving beacon in a world where everything else navigates without light. Make this mechanically true — encounter frequency tied to light discipline — and your players will make interesting choices unprompted.
- **[OPEN] Do you want light to be scarce?** If yes, magical light sources become premier treasure and the economy of the Undermarch runs on them. If no, ignore this and lean on other pressures.

## 8.3 Insectoid Statblock Sourcing [PROPOSED]

Don't build from scratch. Reskin and re-level:

- **Formians** (hive society, existing PF2e lineage) — closest fit for a caste civilization
- **Ankhravs** — good mid-level bruisers
- **Mukradi, Thriae, various vermin** in Monster Core for the bestiary spine
- Build 3–4 **signature** insectoid statblocks yourself at different levels and reskin everything else. Players remember silhouettes, not stat blocks.

# 9. THE MAP PROGRAM

## 9.1 Draw These, In This Order [PROPOSED]

**Do not start with the world map.** This is the most common worldbuilding mistake and it wastes the most effort. The world map is the least useful thing you can draw right now, because you don't yet know what needs to be on it.

**Map 1 — The Cross-Section of the Fallwater.**
A vertical slice. This is your **most important drawing** and it should be first, because:
- Your setting's defining property is *verticality*, and you cannot design vertically until you've drawn vertically.
- It forces you to answer physical questions you're currently deferring: how deep, how wide, how does anyone get down, where does light stop, where does the river fall.
- It's the single best table prop you will ever make. Players will point at it constantly.
- It's genuinely fun to draw.

**You now have everything this map needs. Here's the brief, pulled straight from locked canon:**

| Element | What's already decided |
|---|---|
| The river | Falls from the surface through the Throat - your central visual (§4.2) |
| The Drowned Chapel | Wedged in the Throat wall, ~40m below the Lip (§12.1) - mark it |
| The winch-tower | Concord's, at the Lip, controlling descent (§11.2) |
| Draft direction | Decide: is the Fallwater inhaling or exhaling? (§3.5) - changes where fog/mist sits on the drawing |
| Light | Real sun down to the Threshold floor, doline-style pockets of green wherever a ledge catches it (§3.3b) |
| The Shelf | Where the shaft meets the first horizontal space - this is where Map 2 picks up |
| The Verge | The edge of anything surveyed - mark it, and mark that everything below is blank |

*Real-world reference:* mine shaft diagrams, karst cross-sections, cenote profiles, oil rig cutaways. In cave-survey vocabulary, what you're drawing is called a **projected elevation** or **profile** - the vertical companion to a plan (top-down) view. Searching that exact term will surface real cave-survey examples to steal composition from.

**Map 2 — The Colony at the Shelf.**
The boomtown. Vertical, built on scaffold and rope, clinging to a shaft wall. Not a top-down village map — an *isometric or sectional* one. This is where your players will spend every non-delving hour, and it should be as characterful as the dungeon.

**Map 3 — The Local Reach (node map, not a survey).**
The Undermarch around your home Undertear. **Draw this as nodes and links, not as an accurate map** — circles for locations, lines for routes, annotations for hazards and travel time. Precise cave cartography is a trap: it takes forever, and at the table nobody navigates a real cave map, they navigate a route.

**Map 4 — One Dungeon Site, in full detail.**
A single Sunfled ruin or Sealing Era fortification. Room-and-corridor, gridded, ready to run. Just one. Prove the pipeline before scaling it.

**Map 5 — The World Schematic. Last.**
And keep it *schematic*: continents in rough mass, the twenty Undertears marked, watersheds indicated (because of §4.2), the surface powers blocked in. Resist detail. Nobody will ever be there.

## 9.2 Analog Kit

- **Isometric grid paper** for the vertical stuff — this is the unlock for cross-sections and the scaffold-town. Ordinary square grid fights you on verticality.
- **Dot grid** for dungeon interiors; less visually noisy than square grid.
- **Fineliners in 3 weights** (e.g. 005 / 01 / 03) — hairlines for hatching, medium for walls, heavy for the frame. Consistent line weight is what makes hand-drawn maps look professional.
- **Tracing paper / layout paper** — draw the rock once, overlay different states (flooded / insectoid-held / ruined). Enormously useful for a campaign where places change.
- **Hachure marks** — the standard cartographic convention for pit edges and drop-offs. Learn these; they'll make your vertical maps instantly readable.

# 12. EARLY DUNGEON SITES NEAR THE FALLWATER

**[PROPOSED - session 5]** Three sites, all reachable from the Fallwater without a long expedition, spanning levels 1-5 and three different modes of play: vertical/environmental, social/tragic, and hazard/negotiation. None require the antagonists to be resolved. Small, deliberate seeds of the deeper mystery are planted in each - nothing that commits you to an answer yet.

## 12.1 The Drowned Chapel — levels 1-2

**Location.** Wedged into the Throat wall of the Fallwater itself, roughly 40 metres below the Lip. Visible from the winch-tower on a still day, swaying slightly in the draft.

**Premise.** The village chapel, caught on a ledge the Throat carved as it tore open. It didn't fall - it's held. The congregation was mid-service. Most didn't get out.

**Shape of the site.** Not a corridor crawl - a climb through a tilted building. Load-bearing pews, a bell that still hangs and will ring if disturbed (a Throat Squall gust could set it off unprompted - see 13.2). Bodies remain; some are recoverable for the Vigil's rites, some show signs that looters have already been through.

**The twist.** A survivor is still alive, having sealed themselves into the sacristy for two months, rationing communion wine and altar bread. How the party handles this - rescue, loot around them, report to a faction - is a strong early character-defining choice, and it's exactly the kind of hard, human-scale decision the grim-survival tone should run on.

**Faction pull.** The Vigil wants the bodies and relics and will pay or grant standing for retrieval. Concord may want the lead roofing and the bell salvaged. A contained, low-stakes introduction to the triangle's competing claims before the party has to navigate it at full scale.

## 12.2 The Hushed of the Undersong — levels 3-4

**Location.** A day or two past the Verge, in a side-passage newly ventilated by the Weeping - sealed with bad air until the Undertears started the world breathing.

**Premise.** A pocket of survivors called **the Hushed** - not fresh refugees, but descendants of something older, cut off since the Sealing Era, who believed they were the last people in the world. Pale, sensitive-eyed, they've survived on cultivated fungus and mineral light for generations. **The new air currents are killing their food supply** - their fungus depended on the old, sealed air chemistry (ties directly to 3.5). The same event that let the party find them is quietly destroying them.

**Shape of the site.** A small settlement, not a combat gauntlet - narrow galleries, fungus terraces going brown, defensive traps built generations ago against something the Hushed call only "the old fear." An elder willing to trade lore for aid, if aid can even be offered.

**Why it matters.** Rich roleplay and moral weight rather than loot. A live thread toward the parked "why did the ways close" question (6.1) - the Hushed may hold oral history nobody living has heard. "The old fear" is a controlled, deniable hint you can develop later without committing to what it means yet.

**Faction pull.** The Vigil would be desperate for contact - proof of doctrine, or a crisis of faith. Concord would want to catalogue or exploit them. The Commons would see kindred desperate people worth aiding. A single dungeon site that every faction wants for a different reason.

## 12.3 The Cinderspore Reach — levels 2-5 (scalable)

**Location.** Near the Verge, along an exhaling Mouth thick with bioluminescence.

**Premise.** A vast, richly productive bioluminescent fungal grove - the first significant renewable food and medicine source found near the Fallwater. Exactly what "Winter Is Two Months Away" (11.4, hook D) needs, and all three factions want it: Concord to toll it, the Commons to survive on it, the Vigil to interpret it as an omen - light where there should be none.

**Shape of the site.** Environmental-hazard-forward rather than monster-forward. Standing drafts move spore clouds through the galleries (13.1). A Blackdamp Pocket sits in the grove's lowest hollow (13.2). Unstable footing on fungal terraces. Beneath the growth, old cultivation terraces from someone much older - and half-buried in one terrace, a tool that matches no known culture. **A second First Sign**, delivered as a detail in a location rather than a single object, for a table that already found the first one and wants confirmation rather than a fresh mystery.

**Faction pull.** A natural site to be hired as guard, scout, or negotiator by any of the three factions - or to broker a three-way deal between them. This is where the Victory Point / Influence subsystem noted in 8.1 would actually earn its keep, if you want a mechanical layer under the negotiation.

## 13.1 Environmental Rules

### The Standing Draft

Every Throat and most major tunnels carry a permanent wind. This is ambient, not a hazard - it applies constantly and shapes how players plan.

| Situation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Moving against the draft | Difficult terrain. In a Throat, **greater** difficult terrain. |
| Ranged attacks along the draft axis | -2 circumstance penalty (-4 in a Throat) |
| Perception checks relying on hearing | -2 circumstance penalty; the draft is loud |
| Non-magical open flame | DC 15 flat check each round or it goes out. DC 20 in a Throat. |
| Flying, Medium or smaller | Acrobatics vs the draft's DC to hold position; otherwise pushed 10 feet per round |
| Scent-based tracking | Doubled range downwind, impossible upwind |

**Direction matters.** Each Undertear is either **inhaling** (downdraft) or **exhaling** (updraft), and can flip seasonally. Note it on every map.

### Bad Air

Miner's vocabulary, used in-world by anyone competent:

| Term | What it is | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Blackdamp** | Oxygen-depleted air | Suffocation, fatigue - see 13.2 |
| **Firedamp** | Methane | Explosive. An open flame is a mistake. |
| **Stinkdamp** | Hydrogen sulfide | Poison damage; deadens the sense of smell before it kills |
| **Whitedamp** | Carbon monoxide | Poison; no warning signs at all |

**The breathable frontier** advances deeper every month as the Undertears ventilate the deep. Beyond it, air is a resource you carry.

## 13.2 Hazards

### THROAT SQUALL - HAZARD 5
*Complex, Environmental*

**Stealth** DC 20 (expert) - a pressure drop in the ears and a rising howl precede it by a few seconds
**Description** The air column surges as thermal fronts collide kilometres below, or as the draft scrapes past the edge of something that will not let it in.
**Disable** Survival DC 22 (read the warning signs and brace the party) or Athletics DC 24 (fix and secure a line before it hits)
**AC** 22; **Fort** +14, **Ref** +8
**Immunities** object immunities; can't be attacked or damaged

**Squall** [reaction] **Trigger** A creature enters the Throat, or the squall begins. **Effect** The hazard rolls initiative.

**Routine** (2 actions) The squall uses both actions each round for 3 rounds, then subsides.

*First action -* **Surge** Each creature in the Throat attempts a DC 22 Fortitude save.
- **Success** Pushed 5 feet along the draft.
- **Failure** 2d6 bludgeoning damage and pushed 15 feet along the draft.
- **Critical Failure** 4d6 bludgeoning damage, pushed 30 feet, and must succeed at a DC 22 Reflex save or lose its handhold.

*Second action -* **Torn Debris** Rope ends, cargo, scaffold boards and loose stone. One random creature in the Throat takes 3d6 bludgeoning damage, DC 22 Reflex basic save.

**Reset** The Throat squalls unpredictably, roughly once per hour. **Near the edge of the Stillness, roughly once per ten minutes.**

> **Design note.** In a horizontal tunnel this is a nasty but survivable buffeting. In a vertical Throat the push is a *fall*. Same hazard, radically different threat depending on geography - which teaches players to respect the terrain rather than the number. Scale to other bands by adjusting DCs and damage dice; the shape holds.

### BLACKDAMP POCKET - HAZARD 2
*Environmental*

**Stealth** DC 18 (trained) - flames gutter and burn low; a carried bird sickens first
**Description** A hollow where oxygen-depleted air has settled and stayed. Odourless, invisible, and heavier than good air, so it pools in dips, sumps and dead-end chambers.
**Disable** Survival DC 18 (recognise the signs and withdraw before entering) or Crafting DC 20 (rig a draft-line to ventilate the pocket)

**Suffocation** [reaction] **Trigger** A creature enters the pocket or begins its turn there. **Effect** The creature attempts a DC 18 Fortitude save.
- **Success** No effect, but the creature is aware something is wrong.
- **Failure** The creature is **fatigued** and cannot recover from this condition until it has spent 10 minutes in clean air.
- **Critical Failure** As failure, and the creature falls **unconscious** and begins suffocating (see the suffocation rules).

**Reset** The pocket refills within an hour unless the chamber is permanently ventilated.

> **Design note.** This is your quiet attrition hazard - it does not deal damage, it *spends* the party. Fatigued characters make worse decisions three rooms later. Use it to make air a planning problem rather than a damage problem.
