Names for the cataclysm

Open

Three names in tension: the Weeping (folk, mournful), the Opening (charter/official), the Unroofing (deep-dweller). Which name a character uses tells players who they are, for free. None of these are chosen yet — "the Weeping" is in use throughout as a working default.

§3World Structure

The Weftways

Open — evaluating

Ancient horizontal tunnels connecting the archipelago's deep regions to each other, built over centuries. The insectoids' real superpower isn't destruction, it's connection — this is what makes a 20-front invasion logistically possible, and hands the players a transit system made of enemy infrastructure once captured.

Why they were built: nothing to do with the surface, originally. The insectoid homeland is one isolated deep region among several. The Weftways connected their civilization to other habitable pockets underground, for trade and expansion — internal logistics, built for entirely mundane (to them) reasons.

How: slow, patient, conventional tunneling by the same worker and taskmaster castes that run the rest of their society — magically assisted, but methodical. A fundamentally different technique from the ritual that tore the Undertears, which was fast, violent, and spent nearly a generation of mages in one go.

When, relative to everything else: finished long before the current crisis — the Weftways predate the heat failure entirely. The Undertears are recent, a single costly ritual roughly two months ago, once the crisis forced the issue. The Weftways weren't built for war; once the need to reach the surface arose, the insectoids realized existing infrastructure could move an army fast enough to stage a genuinely simultaneous invasion. Opportunism, not a decades-long plan.

Why not just tunnel upward the same slow way? Time. The Weftways took centuries because centuries were available; the heat crisis is measured in years or decades, far too fast for Weftway-pace digging to finish before the homeland freezes. They needed a fast method because the slow method they already knew was too slow for the actual deadline.

Why twenty at once, when they didn't know anyone lived up there? Not tactics — logistics. Twenty openings is evacuation throughput plus engineering redundancy. You cannot move an entire civilization up a single shaft, and with one irreversible ritual and no ability to repeat it, you don't bet everything on one shaft that might collapse or hit an impassable stratum. Neither reason requires knowing anything about the surface.

Working name for the network: the Weftways / the Sutures / the Stitch — name still unconfirmed.

The Underglass

Builds on your ideaOpen

Answers a mass-conservation problem: a shaft on this scale displaces an enormous volume of rock, which has to go somewhere. Compression is the mechanism — "much like how compressed coal is converted to diamonds." The ritual doesn't excavate and then dispose of spoil; the compression is the digging. Rock ahead of the bore gets crushed into something far denser, which becomes the floor the ritual is working against.

No figure is attached to the displaced volume, deliberately. An earlier draft carried a specific number that was wrong by orders of magnitude once the Throat's real width was settled at forty kilometres. The mechanism stands on its own; if a number is ever needed, derive it then.

Self-terminating by design: the compressed material gets harder with depth, until it becomes harder than the ritual's own compression can further affect. The boring doesn't stop because something else blocked it — it stops because it ran into its own byproduct. No one can simply repeat the ritual harder and go deeper.

Solves the visibility question for free: you can't see the planet's core because the Root is the Underglass — a solid floor, not a long empty drop.

Scope: per-Undertear, not a shared planet-wide shell. Twenty independent floors, no structure connecting them.

What can be found inside it: total compression destroys shape, so nothing survives intact. What remains is a density discontinuity — different materials (stone, wood, flesh, air) compress at different rates under the same instantaneous force, leaving permanent variation in the Underglass's mineral banding at the boundaries where those materials used to be. Miners occasionally find a warped, abstract smear roughly the size and rough shape of whatever was there, distorted past clean recognition — more unsettling for being almost, not fully, legible.

See also: connects to the Root Entity, which may sit at or be embedded in the Underglass rather than beyond it.

§5The Antagonists

Species Basis

Open

Homebrewed on Formian caste architecture (real castes, existing statblocks to reskin) with Thriae magical culture layered on top (spellcasting as institution, not talent). Includes a fungus-farming hook tying their agriculture directly to the deep mushroom forests, and a Golarion reference point (the Vaults of Orv) worth mining for engineered-biome imagery. Never explicitly confirmed.

The Stillness — mechanism & detail

Builds on your idea, detail unconfirmed

The core concept — a fading protective enchantment forcing eventual migration — is confirmed (see Established Canon). What's still open: the specific mechanism (it runs on deep heat, which the Root Entity is stealing — one cause, two symptoms), and a way for players to detect it (the boundary is receding and measurably so).

Their flaw: they never scouted before committing everything to an irreversible ritual — institutional overconfidence and centuries of unchallenged assumption about what the surface even was. See the invasion timeline.

The Invasion Timeline

Builds on your ideaOpen

The insectoids didn't know the surface was inhabited when the ritual fired. The sequence:

WhenWhat
Centuries agoThe Weftways built — trade and expansion between underground regions. Nothing to do with the surface.
LaterThe Root Entity begins draining deep heat. The Stillness starts receding. The homeland begins to die.
RecentlyThe mage castes develop the Undertear ritual as an evacuation plan, aimed at a surface they believe is lethal but endurable — and certainly empty.
~2 months agoThe ritual fires. Twenty shafts open at once, because that's what evacuating a civilization requires.
Immediately afterThe surprise. The surface is not only inhabited, its people start climbing down within weeks — organized, armed, expansionist.
NowThe insectoids scramble to improvise a military they never had and never planned for.

Why they expected an empty surface. As a sun-averse deep species, their records and myth would describe the surface as a lethal burning void — not unoccupied, but uninhabitable, somewhere nothing could survive. They emerged braced for a wasteland to endure. They found farms, roads, and cities in what their people believe is a death-world.

What this means for play: the ~10-month clock isn't a countdown on a long-laid scheme — it's how long a civilian civilization needs to build an army from nothing. The First Sign is reconnaissance, not a vanguard: whatever took Vance is trying to understand what it's run into, and it's as unsettled as the colonists are.

Why Hasn't a High-Level Hero Fixed This?

Open

Four combinable answers: the surface powers are fighting each other instead of the dark; power doesn't solve search at planetary scale; the first wave of heroes went down and didn't come back (their gear is now treasure); the threat isn't legible yet. Recommended weighting toward the first — surface disunity as the real antagonist.

§6The Dead Civilizations

The Era Stack — the Builders, the Sunfled, the Sealing Era, the Aftermath — is now confirmed and has moved to Established Canon, along with the descent of Corrach-Nom's dwarves and gnomes from Aftermath survivors. One gap in it is deliberately unanswered and tracked on Open Questions: how anyone left the deep at all, given the ways were sealed.

§7The Clock

Exploration as the counter-invasion

Open

Megadungeon play (exploratory, digressive) and a hard invasion deadline (urgent, focused) fight each other by default. The fix: make the dungeon itself the source of the counter-invasion — Weftway maps, Sealing Era records, a defector — so digression isn't wasted time. An 8-segment escalating clock, trigger-based rather than calendar-based.

§8PF2e Mechanics

Attrition, Supply & Sourcing

Open

PF2e's fast-recovery design (Focus Points, free Treat Wounds) defeats classic megadungeon attrition by default. Proposed fix: environmental pressure over resource-tracking, an abstract Supply pool gating rest rather than nerfing healing, and reskinned Formian/Ankhrav/Mukradi statblocks instead of building insectoids from scratch. None of this has been built out yet.
