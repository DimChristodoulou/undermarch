Elder Cael Fenn

NPCProposed

Head of the Vigil of the Weeping, a schismatic order declared heretical by the mainstream Church for treating the Weeping as scripture-worthy. Isolated, and therefore willing to cut uncomfortable deals for protection. Not resident at the Fallwater; Cotter answers to him at a distance.

Rank note: Warden is the Vigil's ordinary rank, held by Cotter and by every chapter-house medic. Elder is the order's head. Keep the two distinct at the table.

Hook: the Vigil's scholars catalogue thermal and atmospheric anomalies obsessively without understanding them — the in-fiction route by which the party could learn about the inverted gradient.

Renamed 2026-09-07, was Warden Cael Ashgrove. Too close to Surveyor Nen Ashfeld, and a second Warden alongside Cotter.

"Ninefinger" Sarn

NPCProposed

Independent guide, unaffiliated with any of the three factions. Went deeper than anyone, early, and lost fingers to frostbite somewhere that should have been getting hotter. Doesn't know why. Trusts nobody's authority but his own experience.

Hook: a living, walking clue to the inverted gradient — his scars are evidence before any instrument confirms it.

The ShallowsDenizens

The Hushed

DenizensProposed

Isolated survivors descended from the Sealing Era, cut off long enough to believe they were the last people in the world. Pale, sensitive-eyed, sustained for generations on cultivated fungus and mineral light. Not hostile by default — a people to negotiate with, not fight.

The cruelty of the timing: the same new air currents that let the party find them are killing the fungus their whole food supply depends on. They call something only "the old fear" — a controlled, deniable thread toward the parked "why did the ways close" question — see Established Canon → Unknown Knowledge.

Found at: The Hushed of the Undersong, below.

Near the FallwaterDungeon Sites

The Drowned Chapel

DungeonLocked

Levels 1–2. The village chapel, wedged at roughly thirty degrees into a Mouth the Throat cut as it tore open, ~40m below the Lip. The congregation was mid-service. Forty-one of them died in the fall. The building is the dungeon: a tilted floor rather than a corridor crawl, reached by traversing sideways off the descent line, with a bell that still hangs and could ring if disturbed.

The twist: three of the congregation survived the fall — luck, nothing more — barricaded themselves in the sacristy, and counted thirty-one days on the plaster. Then they left, deliberately, through the altar-breach into the caves behind, taking the lamp oil. They made a camp four hundred metres in and lived there for weeks. Then something came, and they are gone, and the lamp oil is still sitting by the cold fire.

Why it matters: the chapel's dead are the cause of the sickness at the Lip, and the missing three are the hook that keeps working. The party can still attempt that rescue, and the answer is not down here.

Faction pull: the Vigil wants the bodies recovered and given rites, and the censer back; the Compact wants the cause stopped and reported to them first; the Sounding Line wants air and condensate samples taken in sequence on the way down.

Corrected 2026-09-07. Previously described one survivor, still alive, sealed in the sacristy and rationing communion wine. Adventure 01 is authoritative: three survivors, gone before the party arrives.

The Hushed of the Undersong

DungeonProposed

Levels 3–4. A day or two past the Verge, in a side-passage newly ventilated by the Weeping. Home of the Hushed — a small settlement site, not a combat gauntlet: narrow galleries, browning fungus terraces, generations-old traps built against "the old fear."

Faction pull: the Vigil wants contact for doctrine; the Compact wants them counted, registered and controlled; the Sounding Line wants them studied.

The Cinderspore Reach

DungeonHabitatProposed

Levels 2–5, scalable. A vast bioluminescent fungal grove near the Verge — the first significant renewable food and medicine source found near the Fallwater, and exactly what the "Winter Is Two Months Away" pressure needs. Environmental-hazard-forward rather than monster-forward: standing drafts move spore clouds, a Blackdamp Pocket sits in the lowest hollow, unstable fungal terraces underfoot.

A second First Sign: beneath the growth, old cultivation terraces from someone much older, and a half-buried tool matching no known culture.

Faction pull: all three factions want it — for provisioning, for survival, for omen-reading. A natural three-way negotiation site.

Read these aloudPhysical Phenomena

Atmospheric and physical effects, kept separate from hazards below because they're not obstacles to overcome — they're things to describe. Pick one when the party needs to feel the scale of the place.

The Vanishing Waterfall

PhenomenonYour ideaLocked

Water falling into an exhaling Undertear shreds into droplets and hits terminal velocity within the first 100–200 metres — the same mechanism that turns Angel Falls to mist in the dry season. Here it meets the shaft's rising warm updraft head-on, stalls mid-air, and evaporates into it rather than continuing down. The vapour climbs, cools, and condenses again near the surface — as the permanent fog already sitting over an exhaling Undertear's Lip.

At the table: the river doesn't reach the deep. Part of it just turns around and rains fog on the people who dammed it. Confirmed for the Fallwater — read this aloud the first time the party looks down the Throat.

See also: Air & the Standing Drafts, the shaft depth this depends on.

The Frost Veil

PhenomenonProposedOpen

Deep in any sufficiently long shaft, at the exact boundary where the hot Mid Reaches meets the cold Under Deeps, any humidity that survived the hot band's evaporation hits its dew point and crystallizes in open air — a curtain of falling ice and rime hanging at the gradient reversal itself, in a stretch of the shaft nobody but the insectoids has ever reached.

At the table: a striking, wrong-feeling image to signal the party has crossed into genuinely hostile depth — visually and thematically the opposite of the Vanishing Waterfall's melancholy fog.

The Downdraft Furnace

PhenomenonProposedOpen

The mirror case, for inhaling Undertears. Air pulled down a shaft compresses as it descends, and compression heats gas — so an inhaling Undertear can run unexpectedly hot partway down, even above the naturally hot Mid Reaches band.

At the table: a hazard signature for inhaling Undertears distinct from the Vanishing Waterfall — this one just cooks things that fall in. Good contrast if you ever build a second Undertear with the opposite draft direction.

The Throat Squall

PhenomenonProposed

The standing draft isn't smooth wind — it's turbulent pulses. The Mid Reaches supply the driving heat, but air moving through kilometers of irregular shaft, fed by side-passages at different temperatures and squeezed through changing widths, doesn't arrive steady. It arrives in bursts, the way a real chimney can roar unpredictably, or the way mountain passes produce sudden violent gusts (williwaws — cold air pooling, then releasing through a constriction, are the closest real-world parallel). A squall is one of those pulses breaking loose.

Not confined to the open Throat. Any passage connected to the active airflow can squall — and narrower passages are often worse, not safer. The same volume of air forced through a smaller opening speeds up (the Venturi effect — the same physics that makes a garden hose spray faster with a thumb over the end). A tight side-tunnel feeding off a Throat can turn a moderate draft into a violent one purely by being narrow.

At the table: dead-end chambers with no through-airflow are the exception — nothing pulses through a pocket that isn't part of the circuit. Everything else connected to the draft is fair game, and constriction is a warning sign, not a safety feature.

See also: the mechanical implementation is the Throat Squall stat block below (Hazard 5) — scale DCs and damage up in tight constrictions, down in open caverns. A level-1 downscale is in Adventure 01.

Squall [reaction] Trigger A creature enters the Throat, or the squall begins.

Surge — DC 22 Fortitude. Success: pushed 5 ft along the draft. Failure: 2d6 bludgeoning, pushed 15 ft. Critical failure: 4d6 bludgeoning, pushed 30 ft, DC 22 Reflex or lose handhold.

Torn Debris — one random creature takes 3d6 bludgeoning, DC 22 Reflex basic save.

Reset Roughly once per hour; near the Stillness's edge, roughly once per ten minutes.

In a horizontal tunnel this is survivable buffeting. In a vertical Throat, the push is a fall.

Blackdamp Pocket Hazard 2

Environmental

Stealth DC 18 (trained) — flames gutter low; a carried bird sickens first. Under lantern light there is no flame to read, and the first person through feels stupid before they feel short of breath

Description Oxygen-depleted air, odourless and invisible, pooling in dips and dead ends.

Disable Survival DC 18 (withdraw) or Crafting DC 20 (rig a draft-line to ventilate)

Suffocation [reaction] DC 18 Fortitude. Success: no effect. Failure: fatigued until 10 minutes in clean air. Critical failure: as failure, and falls unconscious, begins suffocating.

Doesn't deal damage — it spends the party. Makes air a planning problem, not a damage problem.

Amended 2026-09-12Light & Sounding Lanterns

Sounding lanterns are infrastructure, and the Line issues them into new ground

MechanicLocked

The ruling. Sounding lanterns burn without fuel and indicate the direction of the surface. They are not common and not for sale; the enchantment is expensive. They have two footprints, and only two. Most are installed infrastructure — bolted at intervals down the Throat to light the most-used routes and to mark the way toward the surface and toward the nearest Mouth. The rest are issued to crews going into new territory, not to keep but to leave: a crew that opens a route bolts its lanterns into it, and the route is lit for whoever comes next. Exploration is how the lit network grows, and the Line pays for it in lanterns.

WhereLight
The Throat, on a major routeLit by installed sounding lanterns. Sight-lines between them. The route to the surface and to the nearest Mouth is legible without a check.
The Throat, off the marked routesDark unless the party were issued lanterns for it. Otherwise your own light, and the draft's DC 20 flat check per round against open flame.
Mouths, side-passages, anywhere past the VergeDark. Nothing is installed yet. Carry your own — and if the Line issued you lanterns, this is where you are expected to leave them.

At the table: a sanctioned expedition into new ground is lit, and the open-flame flat check mostly stops mattering for it. That is deliberate: the pressure worth playing is supply, weight and Athletics, not counting torches. The flat check still bites for anyone working unsanctioned, anyone who lost their issue, and anyone who went further than the lanterns they were given. It remains a quiet piece of faction power — the Line decides who gets to light new ground, and could stop.

Consequence to watch: a party that steals or salvages a sounding lantern — or that keeps an issued one instead of placing it — has committed a serious theft against the faction most likely to buy their survey data, and has left dark a route other people were promised.

Locked 2026-09-07, amended 2026-09-12. The original ruling made lanterns fixed installations only, never sold and never given away. Amended: still not sold, but the Line issues them to crews entering new territory, to be left behind as route markers. The 2026-09-07 correction otherwise stands — lanterns are not "in universal use below ground," and the light economy is not free. See History.
