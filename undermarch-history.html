Process · not canon or gameplay

HistoryEvery correction, superseded idea, and reversed decision. The canon pages state what's true now — this page is the only place that explains what used to be true and why it changed.

Nothing here is current canon. If a "was" and a "now" conflict with anything on another page, the other page wins.

Session 7 — Undertear Depth

Was: the Throat's anatomy text said the open shaft ended after "hundreds of meters to several kilometers," handing off to separate horizontal tunnels below the Shelf.

Why it changed: this directly contradicted Fig. 1 in the master bible, which always depicted a single continuous shaft running through all five depth bands — a contradiction introduced and never caught. You caught it: "I thought that the Undertears are straight holes that are kilometers long... an Undertear is simply a long hole that reaches almost to the Root (40+ kilometers)."

Now: see Established Canon → Undertear Depth.

Session 7/8 — Why the Shafts Don't Collapse

Was: "Magically Held Open" — residual ritual magic alone holds the shafts open against what the rock physically wants to do.

Why it changed: superseded once the mass-conservation question came up — a shaft this size displaces an enormous volume of rock, and "lingering magic" didn't explain where that mass actually went. (The specific figure used at the time, ~30 billion m³, was later retracted — see Session 17 below.)

Now: see Proposed Canon → the Underglass.

Session 8 — What's Preserved in the Underglass

Was: "miners occasionally finding compressed, barely-recognizable silhouettes" — implying identifiable shapes survive the compression.

Why it changed: you pointed out this contradicts the mechanism itself — total compression destroys shape, it doesn't preserve it.

Now: density-discontinuity "ghosts" in the mineral banding, not preserved shapes. See Proposed Canon → the Underglass.

Session 8 — Underglass Scope

Considered: a single continuous compressed shell spanning the whole planet, which would have explained why the Weftways run at a fairly consistent depth.

Why rejected: you preferred not to introduce a phenomenon that needed its own separate explanation. The Weftways' depth got a cleaner, independent explanation instead (see below), so the shared-shell idea wasn't earning its cost.

Now: per-Undertear compression, twenty independent floors, no structure connecting them. See Proposed Canon → the Underglass.

Session 9 — Why Twenty Undertears at Once

Was: framed as a tactical choice — twenty simultaneous openings to prevent any single coordinated surface defense.

Why it changed: this assumes the insectoids know the surface is inhabited and organized enough to mount a defense — knowledge they don't have before the ritual fires. You caught it: "the insectoids don't know that the surface is inhabited, so they wouldn't have any reason to create the Undertears simultaneously... the insectoids were not prepared for armed conflict, and are now taking some time to prepare an army."

Now: twenty openings is evacuation throughput and engineering redundancy, not tactics. See Proposed Canon → the Weftways.

Session 9 — The Insectoids' Central Flaw

Was: "contempt for surface minds" — implying they knew about and dismissed surface peoples before the ritual.

Why it changed: same root problem as above. They can't be contemptuous of a people they don't know exist.

Now: they never scouted before an irreversible commitment — institutional overconfidence, not arrogance toward a known people. See Proposed Canon → the Stillness and the Invasion Timeline.

Session 10 — The Fallwater's Local Factions

Was: three factions scoped to the Fallwater alone — the Concord Company, the Commons of the Fallwater (with a militant splinter, the Undertow), and the Vigil of the Weeping.

Why it changed: the first two read as generic — a toll-collecting company and an informal refugee council — and didn't hold up under scrutiny. Concord's central motive (funding a dam for exclusive descent rights) was an indirect causal chain rather than a graspable reason. Being scoped to one Undertear also meant the party would have to rebuild faction relationships from zero at every new site, with no persistent reputation.

Now: factions are global — present in some form at most Undertear settlements rather than confined to one. The Vigil survives with a small origin fix (an old fringe order with existing chapter-houses, not a schism born in the last two months). Concord and the Commons are replaced by the Sounding Line and the Undertear Compact. See Established Canon → Factions.

Not yet done: Vane, Marrow, and the faction-dependent hooks (A and E) on the Gameplay page still assume the old local factions and need rework — flagged there rather than silently rebuilt, since the new factions are still being refined.

Session 12 — The Catchbasin as Water Source

Was: the Catchbasin at ~300m was described as the Fallwater's only water source and "the most strategically important location in the upper shaft." Adventure 01's crisis was a water-fouling emergency built on that premise.

Why it changed: you caught that it makes no physical sense — the Lip has a river directly at the settlement, and lowering barrels down a shaft is trivially easy compared to hauling water up from 300m below. Water was never scarce at the Fallwater; food and lamp-fuel are the actual scarcities, since nothing grows in the dark and nothing burns down there.

Now: the Catchbasin is a junction and landmark where surviving droplets collect and drainage heads deeper — useful to anyone working below the Shelf, not a colony lifeline. See Points of Interest.

Knock-on: Adventure 01's premise was rebuilt around the Fallwater being an exhaling Undertear. Contamination travels upward — bodies aerosolizing into the updraft, condensing as the Lip's permanent fog, causing respiratory illness in the settlement above while the Shelf below stays healthy. This uses locked canon as its engine rather than contradicting it, and explains why boiling the drinking water accomplished nothing. See Adventure 01.

Session 13 — The Shelf, Retired

Was: Undertear anatomy included a Shelf — a widening partway down the shaft where a second colony could stand — and Adventure 01's central clue depended on a healthy "Shelf colony" three hundred metres below a sick Lip settlement.

Why it changed: you don't want a Shelf at all. Your model is a clean shaft, top to bottom, with at most a small encampment built into a pre-existing cave system near a Mouth — never a second town. One settlement per Undertear, at the Lip.

Now: anatomy is four-part — Lip, Throat, Mouths, Verge. See Established Canon.

Knock-on: Adventure 01's inversion clue no longer works without a second settlement to be healthy. Rebuilt around a single population: case density clusters near the Throat's open mouth and thins with distance across the Lip, rather than splitting between two settlements at two depths. Map Program's Map 2 moved from "the colony at the Shelf" to the Lip settlement itself. See Adventure 01 and Map Program.

Session 15 — Cluster B Reordered from Map 1

Was: the Whistling Galleries at ~470m.

Why it changed: Map 1’s first hand-drawn draft, which places the Petrified Forest above the Galleries. The drawing is the authority on vertical order.

Now: the Galleries move to ~580m, below the Petrified Forest at ~540m, and Cluster B spans 430 to 580m. The Holdfast (~1.7km, Cluster E) was added from the same sheet.

Session 15 — The Holdfast’s Defences

Was: written up as a passive ward — an impassable barrier with no interior to run, whose only function was to be a locked door the party could never open.

Why it changed: misread of the brief. You clarified the tower is held by magical traps and constructs that are still on duty, which makes it a hard target rather than a sealed one.

Now: the door stands open and the garrison holds it. A fixed, non-replenishing count of constructs, tracked publicly, that the Compact has already spent two squads against. See Points of Interest.

Session 16 — The Fallwater’s Governance

Was: the watershed above the Fallwater “answers to no single crown — a half-dozen minor lords and a bankrupt duchy share it, none capable of a full dam alone,” and that vacuum was given as the reason outside powers established themselves there.

Why it changed: the vacuum was doing work the factions didn’t need. All three are global bodies with their own reasons to be present, so none of them requires an absent local authority to explain itself. A governed Fallwater is also more useful: it gives the site an owner who made a choice.

Now: the watershed lies inside Velmack, which governs it in fact as well as in name. Velmack could have dammed the river and chose not to — Xynnar judged an open Undertear worth more than the farmland below, and let the farmland drown. Velmack is also a founding nation of the Compact, so the garrison holding its Lip answers to the Compact and to Velmack at once. See Established Canon → The Fallwater.

Session 16 — “Cairn” and “the Kiln” Were Never Canon

Was: Claude’s working notes carried the planet as having a dual name — Cairn in common use, the Kiln among deep-dwellers — and treated it as established.

Why it changed: it was never established. Both names came from a Claude proposal in the naming session, offered in a table of six options alongside five proposed continent names (Ambry, Marrow, The Scour, Orrun, Sarn). You didn’t take any of them; the continents you settled on were Kethal, Naidrora, Aubannon, Obun, Emmux and Jomoth. The planet name was simply never answered, and a proposal got filed as a decision.

Now: the planet has no name in canon, by your ruling, and nothing in the bible needs one. This is the second logged instance of Claude treating an unconfirmed proposal as settled — the same failure as the earlier Sealing and Undertear-count errors.

Session 16 — Two Sessions of File Work Lost

Was: the site files were assumed current. Two sessions of content had in fact never reached them — the Aug 30 surface-world and Burial Ages pass, and the Sept 1 additions of the Darkview Tombs, Petrified Forest and Holdfast. Only structural changes from those sessions survived: the Atlas itself, the normalized navigation, the glossary anchors.

Why it was caught: a question about why the surface world was missing from the Atlas. Checking the canon page directly showed the bankrupt-duchy text still in place, and the POI page was 44 bytes larger than its known-stale copy — a navigation line, not three new sites.

Now: the site files live in a Google Drive folder that is read at the start of each session, rather than being assumed from an older snapshot. Files carry a dated stamp so a stale copy can be identified without reading it. The lost content has been recovered from the session transcripts. See Open Questions for what remains undecided.

Session 16 — The Darkview Tombs Moved to Cluster A

Was: the Tombs placed at ~490m in Cluster B, opening “where the Sunward branch turns back down and the daylight gives out,” described as the dead of everyone who used the road that is now the Barred Way, and linked in the Atlas as sitting above the Whistling Galleries on Map 1.

Why it changed: wrong on every count. Map 1 puts the Petrified Forest above the Galleries, not the Tombs, and the Tombs are in a Mouth in Cluster A. The deeper error is structural: the Darkview Tombs are established canon, and they had been written on top of the Barred Way, which is proposed. Established canon must not depend on proposed canon, or retiring a proposal takes settled material down with it.

Now: Cluster A, On the Shaft. Depth set provisionally to ~180m, between the Drowned Chapel and the Catchbasin, pending your call. Every link to a proposed site is gone — no Barred Way, no Whistling Galleries. Whose dead fill the niches is left unrecorded rather than attributed to a road that may not survive review. The surviving links are to the Lip and to Warden Mott, whose write-up is internal to the site rather than a dependency on another one.

Session 17 — The Throat's Width, and a Volume Figure Retracted

Was: asked how large an Undertear renders on a continental map sheet, Claude described the shaft as "a few km" wide and concluded the openings were sub-millimetre symbols at 1:10,000,000. The ~30 billion m³ displacement figure in Proposed Canon had been written under the same implicit assumption.

Why it changed: you caught it — the width had been discussed as tens of kilometres from early on, and the player briefing, which is canon, describes an Undertear as a region turned on its side with the far wall a smudge on the horizon. "A few km" was Claude's invention, stated as though it were recalled.

Now: the Throat is on the order of forty kilometres across, and that is stated in Established Canon rather than left to memory. The displacement figure is dropped entirely: a 40km-wide, 40km-deep shaft displaces roughly 50 trillion m³, so the old number was wrong by three orders of magnitude. The Underglass mechanism stands without it. At 1:10,000,000 an Undertear draws as a 4mm ring — a real feature, not a symbol.

Session 17 — Surlund Was Never Dwarven

Was: an early proposal gave Kethal a per-nation ancestry table in which Surlund was 51% dwarven with inherited mining craft, and Velmack 18% dwarven.

Why it changed: you established that dwarves and gnomes are primarily an Obun people. The table was a Claude proposal you never confirmed, so no established canon broke — but it had been carried in working notes as though it had been.

Now: no ancestry percentages exist anywhere in canon. Dwarves and gnomes in Kethal are settled residents rather than foreigners; there is no hostility attached to them. (The "upland and cold" placeholder that replaced the table was itself retired in Session 19 — see below.)

Session 17 — Sounding Line Technology, Scope Narrowed

Was: Claude proposed that the Line's whole engineering base — lanterns and cable both — was Diarchy-derived, and you agreed with "make it canon." The consequence drawn at the time was that Corrach-Nom held leverage over every lift line on the planet.

Why it changed: you narrowed it two messages later: the Diarchy supplied the lantern schematics only, and the cable is the Line's own. The planet-wide leverage conclusion does not survive that and has been dropped.

Now: Corrach-Nom developed the sounding lanterns and handed over the schematics, paid not in coin but in guaranteed grain tonnage and a permanent Diarchy seat in Line survey operations at every Undertear. Line cable is the Line's own work and has never been shared. See Established Canon.

Session 17 — A Canon Contradiction That Didn't Exist

Two follow-up messages were built on that claim, including a proposed rebuild of the Atlas from scratch.

Why it changed: none of it was true. Claude's first read of the folder and of the canon page returned material that did not match what was actually there, and a sync landed mid-conversation on top of that. Session 17 — The Era Stack Promoted

Why it changed: establishing Corrach-Nom's dwarves and gnomes as Aftermath descendants made the stack load-bearing. You confirmed the promotion and chose to leave one gap deliberately open.

Session 18 — The Atlas Carried Two Retracted Claims for a Session

Was: the Session 17 retractions were written into the canon pages but not into undermarch-atlas-data.js. The Atlas's Underglass node still listed "Solves: Mass conservation: ~30 billion m³ of rock" as a feature, and its Sounding Line node still presented sounding lanterns as one of two Line products with no mention of Corrach-Nom. Obun was still "nothing has been built here yet," and the era stack was still tagged Proposed and Open.

Why it changed: the Atlas is a separate data file from the pages it summarises, so a canon correction does not reach it unless someone carries it across. Nothing caught the gap at the time because the canon pages themselves read correctly. Found on the next read of the folder.

Now: the Atlas is in line with Established Canon. The volume figure is gone and the node states the mechanism only, noting that any future number must be derived against the forty-kilometre width; the lantern concession is its own node and the cable is explicitly the Line's own; Obun, Corrach-Nom, the Diarchy and the nine coastal nations are built out, with the GM-only origin and the quiet unit as separate nodes; the era stack is locked. The Glossary got the same pass. See Atlas and Glossary.

Standing risk: this is the second time a change landed in the pages and not in the Atlas. The Atlas will need updating in the same pass as any canon change, not after it.

Session 19 — "Upland and Cold" Retired

Session 19 — The Compact's Founding Located

Was: Established Canon said only that the Undertear Compact "was founded there," meaning on Kethal, with no site.

Considered and rejected: founding it on neutral ground — an island outside every continent — which reads better for a multi-continental institution. Rejected because it contradicts the existing Kethal statement, and you preferred to keep the canon rather than amend it.

Now: founded at Xynnar, in Velmack. This is deliberately awkward for Velmack: every other Kethali nation signed away unilateral control of its openings in a building in Velmack's capital, and Velmack governs the Fallwater in fact regardless. Recorded as a live grievance in Established Canon → Factions.

Session 19 — The Floor's Depth, and a Bad Objection

Was: Claude proposed the buried Burial Age layer at 10–20m and then, when you put it at 200m or more, objected that 200m of ashfall was beyond what volcanism could produce.

Why it changed: the objection was wrong. It measured your mechanism against an ordinary volcanic arc, when a Burial Age is sustained flood-scale volcanism running for decades to centuries — and the real-world equivalents lay down one to two kilometres of cover over millions of square kilometres. Your figure was conservative, not excessive.

Now: the Floor is hundreds of metres deep and varies enormously by terrain — 300m+ in basins, ~40m on plains, effectively nothing on stripped highlands — because primary ashfall is only the first stage and millennia of reworking does the rest. Two consequences were added rather than assumed: the crust subsides under the load, so continents don't gain elevation across cycles; and the Undermarch therefore lies beneath kilometres of stacked Burial Ages, which is the physical explanation for the Era Stack's stratification. See Established Canon → The Floor and the Burial Ages.

Session 19 — The Knot, Retracted

Was: Claude proposed "the Knot," an impassable massif where the Red Peaks and the Asbour Mountains meet at the centre of Kethal.

Why it changed: you checked it against the map. The two ranges do not meet — there is open ground between the southern end of one and the western end of the other. The Knot was invented to explain a feature that isn't on the sheet.

Now: the Varngate Gap — the lowland corridor between the two ranges, the only easy overland route between northern and southern Kethal, held and taxed by Velmack. The gap does more work than the massif would have, and it is what the map actually shows.

Session 19 — The Cassiran Gulf Became a Strait

Was: Claude described a "Cassiran Gulf" as sheltered and shallow and simultaneously the only place deep-hulled grain ships could load — a self-contradiction you caught immediately.

Why it changed: the contradiction was real, and your fix was better than a correction. You moved the feature: a strait rather than a gulf, giving Surlund something to enforce a toll on.

Now: Tenemrea's east coast is shoal and delta for hundreds of kilometres, with Cassira sitting on the one scoured channel and requiring permanent dredging. The Cassiran Strait is a separate feature further south, between the mainland and the head of Surlund's island chain, named for the grain run that made it worth holding. Surlund holds both shores and tolls it; the alternative is rounding the tail in open ocean. See Established Canon → The Coasts and the Strait.

Also dropped: a separate "Surlund Reach" inshore channel proposed alongside it. Redundant once the strait exists, and two named passages in the same water is one too many.

Session 19 — Surlund Did Not Know About the Burial Ages

Was: Claude proposed that Surlund's exposed coastal stratigraphy meant Surlunders had known for two thousand years that the world had ended once, and that the rest of Kethal dismissed it as provincial morbidity.

Why it changed: it breaks canon outright. Nobody on the surface knows about the Burial Ages, and giving a whole nation the answer would have handed the campaign's central geological secret to a player who rolled a Surlunder background. You rejected it in those terms.

Now: the exposures stay, the knowledge does not. Surlund's cliffs show the Floor in cross-section for hundreds of kilometres and mean nothing to anyone — local names are descriptive (the grey course, the drowned wood), and the folklore is treated exactly the way Corrach-Nom treats its own literally-true founding tale. A revelation in plain sight, available to be found later rather than already known. Recorded at Established Canon → The Floor and the Burial Ages.

Session 19 — The Weeping Removed from the Burial Cycle

Was: a draft Kethal timeline ran the continental sequence straight into "2 months ago — the Weeping," implying the Weeping is the culmination or the opening of a Burial Age.

Why it changed: you caught the implication. The Weeping and the Burial Ages share an ultimate cause in the Root, but the Weeping is an insectoid evacuation and the next aggressive burial phase is still one to two centuries out. Placing them in one sequence would suggest a connection to the reader that no character could have and that isn't true.

Now: the Weeping is on the world timeline only, and the Kethal continental timeline stops at the last border adjustment forty years ago. A note on the main timeline states explicitly that the two are not part of one sequence. See Established Canon → Timeline.

Session 20 — Xynnar Was Never Inland

Was: Xynnar was described as "capital, inland, on the Fallwater river" in Established Canon's Settlements and Hubs table, in the Atlas's kethal-settlements and xynnar nodes, and in the Glossary.

Why it changed: you ruled that Xynnar is established as a coastal city. As with "upland and cold," the "inland" wording was never anything you decided — it entered with the settlements table and was carried forward unchallenged across three files, because each of the later files was built from the earlier ones rather than from a decision.

Now: Xynnar is a coastal city on the Fallwater river. See Established Canon → Settlements and Hubs. Corrected in the same pass across Established Canon, the Glossary and undermarch-atlas-data.js, per the standing risk logged in Session 18.

Left open — flagged rather than resolved: three consequences follow from a coastal Xynnar and none of them has been decided, so none has been written. (1) Which coast. Velmack's ports are canonically Kessek Roads in the west and Draymund in the east, and "ports on both coasts, which no other Kethali nation has" does not account for a third saltwater city. (2) Whether Xynnar is itself a port, or a coastal capital that is not a harbour. (3) What the Fallwater river does below the Undertear. If Xynnar sits on that river and on the coast, the river reaches the sea past a shaft currently described as swallowing it — which would make Xynnar a river-mouth city whose river was taken by the hole it declined to dam.

Session 21 — What Took the Three

Was: Adventure 01 left the three survivors' fate deliberately unexplained. One "First Sign" covered both the mark at B3 and their disappearance, the Atlas named the insectoid scout as what took them, and the passage past B3 was "simply dark and empty."

Why it changed: you ruled that insectoid scouts taking prisoners makes no sense, and players would ask where the passage goes and where the three are, with nothing to answer them.

Now: two layers. The mark is still the First Sign and still unexplained to players; GM-only, it is an insectoid route label reading Passage 26. The taking was a giant wasp, which answered the bell in A11, dragged the three to a sheer drop past B3, and keeps them as egg-hosts in its larder at the new optional area B4. They are alive if the party reach B4 on their first descent; one dies per day after. Updated in the same pass across Adventure 01 (GM Overview, A11, B3, B4, Creatures, XP, Resolution), undermarch-atlas-data.js and Open Questions.

Left open: why the wasp is here, and whether "26" means the insectoids number side routes as well as the Undertears.
