Points of InterestExplorable locations around the Fallwater, arranged by depth and cluster — each with guidance on running it.

The Throat is a vertical cut through a pre-existing cave labyrinth. Its walls are pierced at intervals by Mouths — severed passages ranging from crawlspaces to openings wide enough to sail a barge through. Each major Mouth opens onto a cluster of connected sites. Draw the shaft in profile; hang the clusters off it at their depths.

On the Shaft — 0 to 300m

Visible from the descent lines. Everyone who goes down passes these.

The Weeping Scar

Shaft wall · 0–200m · Threshold

A vertical band of shaft wall, several hundred metres long and perhaps forty wide, where the ritual's compression went wrong. Instead of the clean shear that characterises the rest of the Throat, the stone here is twisted and glassy, folded into shapes that look poured rather than broken — ropes of rock frozen mid-flow, bubbles the size of a fist, surfaces that reflect lamplight like dark water. It is still faintly warm to the touch two months on, and the warmth does not diminish with depth into the rock.

Descent lines detour around it. Pitons won't hold — the surface is too slick, and where a spike does bite, the rock around it crazes and lets go within the hour. This means every person who has ever descended the Fallwater has passed within thirty metres of it and nobody has stood on it.

The Sounding Line wants samples badly enough to pay well. Anyone who takes one discovers the fragment stays warm in a pack, indefinitely, and that a chip of it held in darkness shows a faint internal grain that was not visible in lamplight.

Running it

A set piece, not a dungeon — one extended scene
Level

Time
Half a session

A climbing and rigging problem with a payoff at the end
Mechanics
Athletics/Acrobatics climbing chain; anchors that fail on a delay rather than immediately; a scaled-down Throat Squall (drop to Hazard 2 for level 1–3: DC 17, 1d6/2d6 damage) is devastating here because there is nothing to hold

The turnNothing attacks. The tension is entirely environmental and the reward is entirely informational — which makes this a good early lesson that the Undermarch punishes carelessness without needing a monster.

The Drowned Chapel

Mouth · ~40m · Threshold · Levels 1–2

The Weeping sheared through the edge of a rim village. Most of the chapel is still up on the Lip, sliced open like a dollhouse. The rest fell forty metres and jammed sideways into the mouth of an intersected cave passage, where it now sits wedged at roughly thirty degrees, held by the rock it is lodged in rather than resting on anything.

Inside, the floor is a slope. Pews have slid and stacked against the downhill wall in a tangle of splintered oak. The font has emptied itself across the flagstones. Everything loose has migrated to the low corner — hymnals, candlesticks, coins from the collection box, and the congregation, who were at service when it happened.

Condensation runs constantly down the walls, because the Fallwater exhales humid air past the Mouth day and night. That water pools in the low corner, sits among the dead, and drains out through a crack in the downhill wall into the cave system beyond — which is why what happens in this room does not stay in this room.

Behind the altar, the chapel's back wall has broken open against the cave passage it is wedged into. The passage continues into unmapped dark.

Running it

Scale
Small — nave, sacristy, altar-breach, plus whatever you build behind it
Level
1–2
Time
One session as written; two if the cave behind is developed
Structure
Linear with one gated room (the sacristy) and one exit onward
Mechanics
The tilt is the terrain: treat downhill movement as normal, uphill as difficult terrain, and any prone creature slides. Rotting pews are unstable ground. Zombie shamblers (level −1) in the low corner work at level 1; a rat swarm (level 1) is the alternative if you want no undead yet

The turnThe sacristy is sealed from the inside. Whoever did that survived the fall. Two months is far too long to survive on communion bread and wine — so either they got out through the altar-breach into the caves and are still back there, or you decide what living that long actually cost them. Both are grim. Pick deliberately.

The Catchbasin

Mouth · ~300m · Threshold

The Vanishing Waterfall mostly becomes mist, but not all of it. What survives the fall collects here: a natural rock bowl roughly the size of a village square, fed by a constant heavy drip out of the darkness above that never stops and never quite becomes rain. The sound is enormous and unvarying. People who work here for a season go partially deaf and learn to read lips.

Nobody drinks from it. The Lip has a river — that is what the Fallwater is named for — and lowering barrels down a shaft costs almost nothing, so the settlement's water comes from above like everything else. The Catchbasin's water is incidental, and the colony's real scarcities are food and lamp-fuel, neither of which can be found down here.

What makes the Catchbasin matter is drainage and geography. Its overflow runs out through a slot in the eastern wall and down into the cave systems below, which makes this the point where the Fallwater's water stops falling and starts going somewhere — and passages lead away from it in three directions, following that drainage. For anyone working this deep in the Threshold, it is the last reliable free water before the Verge. For everyone else it is a junction and a landmark, permanently damp, permanently loud, permanently cold, and completely indefensible against anything arriving from the cave side rather than the shaft side.

Running it

Scale
A hub, not a dungeon — one large chamber with three exits
Level
Any; it is a crossroads
Time
Recurring location; visited many times
Structure
Use it as the Threshold's junction. Most Threshold delves should pass through it
Mechanics
The noise imposes a −4 circumstance penalty to Perception checks relying on hearing and makes conversation above a shout impossible. Stealth is trivially easy here — for everyone, in both directions

The turnEverything that drains out of the upper Throat passes through this room on its way deeper. Whatever is happening above arrives here first, and whatever is coming up from below arrives here last.

The Sunward Mouths — ~450m

A branch trending upward and outward toward the surface. Daylight reaches parts of it. The only cluster in the Undermarch where anything is pleasant.

The Skywell

Sunward Mouths · ~450m · Threshold

A cavern with a genuine sinkhole open to the sky — a natural doline, formed over millennia, nothing to do with the Weeping. Real sunlight falls in a shifting column onto a floor thick with green: ferns, moss, saplings grown leggy and pale reaching for the light, a tangle of vines down one wall where seeds have been blowing in for centuries. Birds nest on the ledges and their noise fills the chamber. There is a smell of rain and rot and growing things.

For a party that has been in the dark, arriving here produces a physical reaction. That is the point of the room.

Which is exactly why it is contested. It is the only reliable safe rest in the Threshold, the only place to grow anything, and the only route to the surface that does not involve the winch-towers and the tolls they carry. Somebody is always already here.

Running it

Scale
Medium — one great chamber plus ledges, the sinkhole climb, and side alcoves
Level
1–4
Time
Recurring; a destination and a base
Structure
Social and territorial rather than combat-first. Who is here, and what do they want?
Mechanics
Full daylight in the column, dim light at the fringes, darkness beyond. Resting here should grant a real, named benefit — otherwise the players will not feel what the room is for

The turnGive it away to someone else first. The party finds the one good place in the dark, and it is occupied. Everything that follows — negotiation, sharing, eviction, murder — tells you who this party is, at level 1, in a room that costs nothing to build.

The Barred Way

Sunward Mouths · ~430m · Threshold

Past the Skywell the passage climbs, widening, its floor worn smooth in a way that only comes from generations of foot traffic. This was a road. It went to the surface. It does not any more.

The blockage fills the passage floor to ceiling: a chaos of collapsed ceiling stone and unmistakably worked masonry, thoroughly intermixed. Someone quarried, cut, and set those blocks — the tool marks are plain and the mortar is real. Something also brought down an enormous quantity of ceiling. Which happened first cannot be determined from the debris, and this matters enormously.

If the wall came first, someone deliberately closed this road and the collapse is later decay. If the collapse came first, someone found a natural disaster and reinforced it, making sure it stayed shut. The first reading is a decision. The second is a confirmation. The party will argue about this, and the evidence genuinely supports both.

There are dozens of these scattered through the upper Undermarch. Nobody has catalogued them.

Running it

Scale
Small — a single site, but one the party will return to
Level
Any
Time
An hour of play; longer if they attempt excavation
Structure
An investigation with no solution. The site poses a question
Mechanics
Lean on Crafting, Society, and Lore rather than Perception — this is expertise, not searching. Reward good rolls with better ambiguity, not answers: an older mortar layer beneath a newer one, tool marks in two different styles

The turnDo not resolve this. Why the ways closed is deliberately undetermined. The Barred Way exists to make the players want to know — hand them a conclusion and you have spent a mystery you have not written yet.

The Whistling Galleries

Sunward Mouths · ~470m · Threshold

A warren of narrow interconnected tubes, none wider than a person, running for a kilometre or more through the rock between the Sunward Mouths and the shaft. The standing draft moves through all of them at once and the entire system sounds — a continuous shifting chord, some notes clear and flute-like, others so low they are felt in the sternum rather than heard.

Colonists navigate it by ear. Each junction has its own note, and the guides who work it have names for them. Getting lost is a matter of losing the tune.

The chord changes when a Throat Squall is building, minutes before it arrives — a rising, souring shift that anyone who has spent a week here recognises instantly. This makes the Galleries the closest thing the Fallwater has to a storm warning, and the reason the guides who work them are worth what they charge.

Prolonged exposure to the infrasound is genuinely unpleasant: nausea, pressure behind the eyes, and a persistent sense of being watched. Vigil accounts attribute the last to something present. The Sounding Line attributes it to the frequency. Both are documented; only one is being investigated.

Running it

Mechanics
Track the tune rather than mapping the tunnels. Perception to distinguish notes; a guide or notes from one turns a hard problem into a manageable one. Extended exposure: a Fortitude save every hour or become fatigued, with the sensation of being followed as flavour that may or may not be real

The turnThe chord sours. The party has three minutes to reach a chamber wide enough to brace in, and they are in tubes barely wider than their shoulders. Narrow passages make squalls worse, not better.

The Lantern Reach — ~700m

The largest Threshold cluster and the historical heart of the upper band. Home to people who were trapped down here when the ways closed — not the Sunfled, who chose to descend, but ordinary surface folk caught on the wrong side.

The Lantern Warren

Lantern Reach · ~700m · Threshold · Levels 2–4

A settlement carved into a cave system across what must have been many generations. Every aspect of its architecture is organised around one problem: light, and the management of it. Walls are inlaid with polished mineral to bounce and redirect flame. Channels are cut through rock at careful angles to carry glow from a lit chamber into an unlit one. Doorways are set off-axis so that light turns corners. And everywhere — in every room, along every corridor, at every junction — small niches for lamps, in their thousands.

The chambers nearest the entrance are the finest: polished to a mirror finish, niches carved with real craft, some still holding lamps with residue in the bowl. Move inward and the workmanship declines. The polish gets rougher. The niches get cruder and more numerous, crowded together as if quantity might substitute for quality. Deeper still, the polish stops entirely, the walls are bare rock, and the niches are simply gouges.

In the deepest chambers there are no niches at all.

The dead here do not rest. They cluster where light falls and will not enter unlit rooms — which is a tactical fact, a navigation aid, and the single grimmest piece of characterisation available in the Threshold.

Running it

Scale
Large — a genuine multi-session dungeon, thirty-plus chambers
Level
2–4
Time
Two to four sessions
Structure
Concentric. Depth into the Warren maps onto the decline of its people — the dungeon is a timeline you walk through
Mechanics
Make light a resource with teeth here specifically. The undead's aversion to darkness inverts normal dungeon logic: the safe route is the dark one, and the safe route is also the one where the party cannot see. Skeleton guards (level 2) and zombie shamblers (level −1) in numbers

The turnThe party works out the rule — they cluster in the light — and then finds a chamber where the dead are standing in total darkness, waiting. Something changed down here before the end, and that chamber is where it started.

The Sunken Cathedral

Lantern Reach · ~700m · Threshold · Levels 3–4

Raised by the trapped population at the height of their strength, before things went bad — an enormous vaulted nave cut from living rock, its ceiling lost in darkness, its pillars carved to resemble trees nobody in the third generation had ever seen. It is a cathedral built underground to worship the sun.

The great rose window is carved into solid stone with nothing behind it. It has never admitted light and was never intended to. It is a picture of a window.

The iconography is the site's real content, and it degrades across the building's timeline. The earliest carvings — near the entrance, cut when the trapped generation still contained people who remembered daylight — are accurate: recognisable sun, recognisable sky, plants with correct leaves. Move deeper and later, and the work is done from description rather than memory. The sun acquires a face. The sky becomes a solid ceiling with the sun set into it like a lamp in a niche, because a lamp in a niche is what the carvers actually knew. The plants become ornamental spirals.

The last carvings, in the apse, depict a great burning eye in a vault of stone, surrounded by worshippers holding lamps up toward it as if feeding it.

The crypt below is sealed, and its seal was made from the inside.

Running it

Scale
Large — nave, transepts, apse, bell chamber, and the sealed crypt
Level
3–4 above; the crypt can be pitched anywhere
Time
One to two sessions above, plus the crypt
Structure
Open exploration above, hard gate at the crypt
Mechanics
The carvings are the dungeon's information delivery. Reward Religion, Society, and Crafting checks with progressively more disturbing readings of the same wall. Vertical space matters — the bell chamber and upper galleries are reachable and hold the best-preserved early work

The turnThe crypt's seal was made from inside, by people who could have left and chose not to. Whatever is behind it, they went to considerable effort to make sure it stayed with them. Decide what that means before your players get there, because they will ask.

The Chandelier

Lantern Reach · ~750m · Threshold

A gypsum chamber where crystal formations hang from the ceiling in masses metres across, grown over hundreds of thousands of years from mineral-saturated water. Naica in Mexico is the real precedent, and the real thing is stranger than most invented caverns. Beams the width of a body, translucent, faintly milky, catching and splitting any light brought into the room.

Every surface is a blade. The formations shatter under weight, and the fragments are worse than the intact crystal. The acoustics are catastrophic — a shout brings down a section of ceiling, and a section of ceiling weighs several tonnes and arrives as a thousand pieces of glass.

The trapped population harvested here. Crystal catches and spreads flame better than anything else available, and the Lantern Warren's mirror-inlay came from these walls. The harvested sections show tool marks alongside natural growth, and the harvesting stops abruptly partway up one wall — mid-cut, tools still in place at the bottom of the chamber where they were dropped.

The turnCombat here is a mistake and the party should realise it one round too late. This is the room that teaches them not every problem is solved by fighting in it.

The Deep Threshold — 800m to 1km

Below the Lantern Reach, the caves get older and less human. The last band before the Shallows.

The Hollow Spire

Deep Threshold · ~850m · Levels 3–5

A tower built down the inside of a natural vertical shaft — floor after floor cut into the rock wall around a central void, connected by a spiral of stairs with no railing, descending some two hundred metres into the dark. Dropped objects fall a long way and are not heard to land.

Its builder was neither Sunfled nor one of the trapped. The construction predates both by a margin nobody can estimate, and the architecture matches nothing else in the band: proportions slightly too tall, stairs slightly too shallow, doorways that are the wrong shape for comfortable passage without being obviously inhuman.

Constructs still patrol it. They follow routines whose purpose is not obvious, carrying out repairs, replacing components, sweeping floors that no longer need sweeping. They are not hostile by default — a party can walk past one and be ignored entirely. They are extremely hostile to specific things, and determining what those things are, before triggering them, is the actual puzzle of the site.

The upper floors are observation: instruments aimed upward and outward, notes in a hand nobody can read. The lower floors are containment. The distinction is unmistakable, and the containment floors are where the constructs are concentrated.

Running it

Scale
Large — vertical dungeon, twelve-plus floors
Level
3–5
Time
Two to three sessions
Structure
Strictly vertical and mostly linear, with the option to descend fast via the central void at considerable risk
Mechanics
The constructs are a rules puzzle, not a fight. Establish their triggers early through observation and let the party exploit them. Fighting one should be survivable at level 4; fighting three is not

The turnThe party works out what the constructs attack — and realises the criteria describes something that has not been seen in the Undermarch for a very long time. The Spire was built to watch for something, and it is still watching.

The Silk Deeps

Deep Threshold · ~900m · Levels 3–5

A cave system given over entirely to an arachnid ecology that has had a very long time to develop without interference. Sheet webs span galleries wall to wall, layered dozens deep, old webbing beneath new until the floor is a metre of compacted silk that gives underfoot like moss. Silk-wrapped bundles hang in numbers that suggest something has been feeding extremely well for a very long time.

The web architecture is navigable if you understand it. There are load-bearing lines and there are trap lines, and the difference is visible to anyone who takes the time to look — thicker, drier, differently anchored. Moving on the right lines is quiet. Moving on the wrong ones announces the party to everything in the system.

At the centre is something whose web has structural columns.

No history, no lore, no civilisation. Sometimes a place should just be dangerous.

Running it

Medium to large — a warren, size to taste
Level
3–5

Stealth-forward exploration with an escalating threat, or a straight gauntlet if the party is loud
Mechanics
Nature or Survival to read the web layout. Success means silent movement; failure means the whole system knows. Giant spiders (level 1) and spider swarms (level 1) as the population, with something considerably worse at the core

The turnThe bundles are not all prey. Some are stored, wrapped, and alive — and one of them has been down here long enough to be worth talking to, if the party can get it out.

Established canon — see the Gameplay page for the full entry. A vast cultivated bioluminescent grove, the first significant renewable food and medicine source near the Fallwater, contested by all three factions. Environmental-hazard-forward rather than monster-forward.

The Fungal Canopy

Fungal Belt · ~1.5km · Shallows · Levels 4–7

What the Cinderspore Reach would be if nobody managed it. Growths tall enough to form a genuine canopy layer — twenty metres and more — with an understory beneath them and a litter layer below that. Bioluminescence in competing colours from competing species: cold blue, sickly green, a deep red that is rarer and always means something. An entire food web operating in permanent shifting twilight.

Nothing here is farmed and nothing here is safe. There is an apex predator, and the local ecology is visibly arranged around avoiding it — clearings that nothing crosses, a band of the canopy where nothing nests, silence that moves.

Navigation is genuinely difficult. The canopy blocks sightlines completely, the light is directional and comes from the wrong places, and the colours shift what you think you are seeing. Compass sense fails. People walk in circles here and do not realise it for hours.

Running it

Very large — a wilderness region, days across
Level

Multi-session; a travel region rather than a dungeon
Structure
Hexploration-style. Adapt the delve-ploration approach from the mechanical layer
Mechanics
Survival checks to avoid circling. The light is a hazard to navigation, not to bodies. Vertical layers matter: canopy travel is faster and more exposed, floor travel is slower and safer

The turnThe party learns to read where the apex predator is by watching what the ecology does — and then finds a region where the avoidance patterns describe something the predator itself avoids.

The Mycelial Nave

Fungal Belt · ~1.8km · Shallows · Levels 4–6

Not a forest. One organism.

A single fungal individual, vast and ancient, whose fruiting bodies have grown into and around the cave structure until the chamber's architecture is the organism — ribbed vaults of pale flesh, columns where a stalk has met the ceiling and thickened over centuries, a floor that is soft and warm and yields underfoot. Real precedent: Armillaria colonies spanning square miles and living for thousands of years.

It responds to disturbance. Slowly — over minutes and hours, not seconds — and chemically, across the whole structure at once. Cut it in one chamber and something changes in a chamber a kilometre away. Whether that constitutes anything resembling awareness is a question the Sounding Line would very much like answered, and their researchers disagree sharply about the ethics of the experiments that would settle it.

It is, incidentally, the largest single living thing anyone in this world has ever encountered.

Running it

Scale
Large — a dungeon whose walls are a creature
Level
4–6
Time
One to two sessions
Structure
Exploration with a rising response. The organism is the dungeon's clock
Mechanics
Track a disturbance counter. Cutting, burning, and taking samples raise it; time and stillness lower it. At thresholds the environment changes — passages constrict, spore density rises, the floor stops being passive

The turnThe party finds a chamber that is deliberately shaped. Not grown — shaped, and recently. Something has been cultivating it, and that something is not the Sounding Line.

The Silt Terraces

Fungal Belt · ~1.9km · Shallows · Levels 3–5

Abandoned Aftermath cultivation: stepped terraces cut into a long slope, once irrigated by a channel that no longer runs. The channel is the first thing anyone notices, because it is beautifully made and completely dry, and following it upstream to find out why is the obvious move.

Tools lie where they were set down. Dwellings stand roofed and empty, their doorways open, their hearths cold and swept. Nothing violent happened here. There are no bodies, no signs of struggle, no barricades. People simply stopped, and left, or dwindled to nothing over a span too gradual to leave a mark.

The terraces themselves have gone wild — the cultivated species still growing, but unmanaged now, spilling across the retaining walls and down the slope in a slow green collapse.

This is the contrast piece to the still-inhabited Hushed settlement. Finding this one first makes meeting living people down here land considerably harder.

The dry channel is the thread — following it upstream is the site's spine and leads to why the water stopped. Reward searching with domestic detail rather than treasure

The turnThe water did not fail naturally. Something upstream diverted it, deliberately, and the work is competent. The Aftermath people did not die — they were moved, or driven, by something that understood irrigation well enough to break it precisely.

The Undersong Cairns

Fungal Belt · ~1.95km · Shallows · Levels 3–4

A cavern where the dead are laid beneath cultivated fungal beds, terraced in low mounds the way the Silt Terraces were terraced for crops — because to whoever built this, they are the same act. A body is a seedbed. Bioluminescent fungus grows deliberately over each burial, and the light it casts is soft, blue-green, and constant, so the whole chamber glows like something seen through water.

The pattern runs opposite to the Lantern Warren's. Near the entrance the cairns are old — the fungal beds long dead, collapsed to gray ash-like mats, the mounds beneath them sunken and bare. Move further in and the beds brighten. Further still and they are visibly tended: trimmed, watered, in places re-seeded with fresh spore. At the back of the chamber are mounds that are days old, glowing as strongly as anything in the Cinderspore Reach.

This is not a ruin. It is a practice, ongoing, and someone is still walking these rows.

Even the healthiest cairns show the same wilting that has reached the Reach itself — patches of grey creeping into beds that should be thriving. Someone has been fighting it: small channels cut to redirect moisture, transplanted clumps grafted onto failing mounds, the same problem the Cinderspore Reach has, tended by hand, on graves, by people trying to save their dead a second time.

In the oldest section, half-collapsed and further back than anything else, a single marker breaks the pattern — not a grave, a ward, cut with the same careful hand as the newest work but plainly ancient. Beneath it, set into the rock rather than grown from it, is a fragment that has not decayed at all in however long it has been there. Nothing else in the chamber has failed to rot. This has failed to rot in the opposite direction.

The turnIf the party lingers past the freshest cairns, someone arrives to tend one. Not a fight — the first living sight of the Hushed, and how the party handles the next sixty seconds decides whether this becomes contact or another vanishing.

The Drowned Quarter — 2 to 3km

Where the Weir runs. The Sunfled built here, and the water took part of it back.

The Weir

Drowned Quarter · ~2.2km · Shallows

A genuine underground river — wide, slow, cold, running horizontally through the Shallows for an unknown distance in both directions. After the vertical drama of the Throat, its horizontality is disorienting. It is navigable by boat, and that single fact changes the entire texture of travel in this band: cargo becomes possible, distance becomes cheap, and the party's relationship to the map changes.

Its banks support their own ecology — pale things adapted to moving water, filter-feeders on the rocks, blind fish in numbers that make them a genuine food source.

It connects several otherwise separate clusters, which makes it the spine of Shallows movement. It is also, inevitably, a chokepoint: whoever holds a landing controls a great deal. And it is the reason the Drowned City is drowned — the Weir's course shifted, at some point, and took a quarter of a settlement with it.

Running it

Scale
A corridor region — the connective tissue of the Shallows
Level
Any
Structure
Travel infrastructure. Use it to make the Shallows feel navigable in a way the Threshold never is
Mechanics
Boats change encounter design — the party is fast, exposed, and cannot easily flee sideways. Current direction matters for travel time and should be noted on your map

The turnThe river goes somewhere. Following it far enough, in either direction, eventually leaves the region the Fallwater can reach — and that is one of the few honest ways out of this Undertear's archipelago island.

The Drowned City

Drowned Quarter · ~2.4km · Shallows · Levels 5–8

A Sunfled settlement, substantial — thousands lived here — now partially submerged where the Weir's course shifted. Colonnades stand in water to their capitals. An amphitheatre is flooded to the third tier, its remaining rows curving up out of the black water like teeth. Statuary stands waist-deep, and the statues are of people, in a style that is recognisably civilised: robes, posture, individual faces, the conventions of portraiture.

That is precisely what makes it unsettling. This is not an alien ruin. It is a familiar one. The party will recognise what a plaza is for, what a public bath is for, what a memorial is for. They will understand this city, and understanding it is worse than not.

The upper levels are dry and structurally sound and can be walked. The lower levels are underwater, and the water is very clear, and things move in them.

Running it

Very large — a city district; run it in sections

5–8

Mechanics
Underwater combat rules matter here and should be prepared in advance. The clarity of the water is a horror device — the party can see what is coming from a long way off and can do nothing about it

The turnThe city was not abandoned in a hurry. The flooding was gradual, and the upper levels show decades of adaptation — raised walkways, blocked doorways, rooms converted to boat landings. They lived with it, for generations, and then they left anyway. Find out what finally did it.

The First Landing

Drowned Quarter · ~2.1km · Shallows · Levels 3–5

The earliest Sunfled burial ground — their first foothold, close to whatever way they used to come down, from generations before the civilisation further below existed.

It is crude. Shallow niches cut into a cave wall with poor tools by people in a hurry. Minimal grave goods: a cup, a knife, in some cases nothing at all. No inscriptions in the earliest section, because whoever was doing the burying either could not write or had no time. These are the burials of refugees, not citizens, and the contrast with the Drowned City's statuary is the entire point of the site.

The chronology is readable in the rock. Move along the wall and the niches get deeper, better cut, better furnished. Inscriptions appear. Then the burials stop — not because the site was abandoned in disaster, but because the population had moved on and downward, and was burying its dead somewhere better.

The earliest graves face upward. All of them. Every subsequent generation faces them a little further from the vertical, until by the last burials they lie flat.

The Ember Shrine

Warm Reaches · ~3.3km · Shallows · Levels 5–7

A temple built directly onto a natural thermal vent, its architecture organised entirely around the heat source. Concentric chambers radiate outward from the vent in rings: the innermost too hot to enter and glazed by centuries of exposure, the next survivable in short bursts, the next comfortable, the outermost merely warm. The gradient is the building's whole logic, and the priesthood's hierarchy was almost certainly mapped onto it.

Whoever built it worshipped warmth, explicitly and without metaphor. The iconography is heat: hearths, coals, hands held out. There is no sun imagery anywhere — these were not people who remembered the surface. They were people for whom the ground itself was the source of life, which is simply, straightforwardly true down here.

In a campaign whose entire antagonist motive is heat scarcity, a shrine to heat costs nothing and pays enormously.

It also functions as one of the few genuine safe rests in the deep Shallows — a warm, dry, defensible structure with clean air — if whatever currently occupies it permits that.

The Mudflats

Warm Reaches · ~3.6km · Shallows

Geothermal activity intensifying as the Mid Reaches approach. Geysers on irregular and unpredictable schedules. Mudpots that belch and settle. Scalding pools with beautiful mineral terracing around their rims, in colours that come from bacteria rather than minerals. Ground that is not reliably solid and gives no warning before it stops being solid.

Crossing requires either local knowledge or a great deal of patience. The safe routes exist but they shift, and last season's markers are worse than useless.

No civilisation, no history, no dungeon. A pure environmental obstacle marking that the band is about to change character — and a filter that keeps the unprepared out of the deep Shallows.

The Regular Passage

Warm Reaches · ~3.8km · Shallows · Story-critical

A tunnel that is wrong.

The bore is perfectly consistent — the same diameter for as far as anyone has followed it, which is several kilometres. There is no natural jointing. No water-carving, no scalloping, no fault to follow. The floor is flat. The walls meet the ceiling at a consistent radius. It curves, occasionally, and the curves have intent: they avoid things, and what they avoid are unstable strata and water tables, which means whoever cut this knew what was there before they cut.

It runs horizontally. It does not connect to anything the Sounding Line has surveyed. Followed in either direction it simply continues, past the limit of anyone's supplies.

And sections of it show repair. Patching, reinforcement, a collapsed stretch cleared and shored — work done in the last few years at most, on a tunnel that is obviously ancient.

This is a Weftway spur, and it is the single most important location in either band for the campaign's main storyline.
